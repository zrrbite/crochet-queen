# Two-tone raglan sweater

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FS3yPJE3CP8"><img src="https://img.youtube.com/vi/FS3yPJE3CP8/maxresdefault.jpg" alt="A fitted crochet pullover with a dark green yoke and sleeves, a cream body, and ribbed collar, cuffs and hem, worn and laid flat" width="480"></a>

*The video's thumbnail; click to watch.*

A top-down raglan pullover in plain double crochet. The yoke is a square
grown outwards from a chained neck ring, increasing at four corners; at the
underarms it's split into a body and two sleeves, all continued in the round,
so there is nothing to sew. Yoke, sleeves and collar are in one colour and
the body in a second, which puts the colour change in a straight line across
the chest. Collar, cuffs and hem are ribbed in post stitches. Intermediate:
the stitches are nothing but dc and post stitches, but it's a garment. You
place the split and the sleeve decreases by trying it on, pick up yarn in
three places, and work a post-stitch decrease at the collar's corners. The
designer pitched it as a first sweater, and it is; a first project it isn't.

## Materials

- Worsted-weight yarn: about 280 g of colour A (yoke, sleeves, collar) and
  110 g of colour B (body). At Wool-Ease's 180 m per 85 g ball that is about
  590 m and 235 m (Ours, from the ball band). For a much larger sweater the
  designer estimates about 1.5 times as much, around 420 g in all. Buy a
  spare ball either way, from the same dye lot.
- 6 mm hook
- Tapestry needle, scissors
- Tape measure, and a locking stitch marker to hold the live loop while you
  try it on (Ours)

## Gauge

15 dc × 9 rounds = 10 × 10 cm, in rounds of dc.

The sizing is done by trying on, so a different gauge doesn't break the
pattern; it changes how much yarn you need and where the try-on points fall.
If you want the measurements in this recipe to come out as written,
[swatch first](../../knowledge/techniques/garments.md#gauge-swatch).

## Before you start

**How it's built.** The yoke is a flat square with a square hole in the
middle for the neck. You chain the neck opening, join it into a ring, and
work rounds of dc outwards, with a ch-1 space at each of four corners. Every
round puts (dc, ch 1, dc) into each corner space, so each side grows by 2
stitches and the corners run outwards as four diagonal lines: the raglan
lines. When the square is big enough that its corners reach a few cm below
your underarms, you fold it so that the two corners on the left meet under
your left arm and the two on the right under your right arm. The front and
back sides of the square are joined to each other at the underarms by a
short chain, and become the body; the other two sides become the tops of the
sleeves. Each sleeve is then worked down in the round from its side of the
square plus the underarm chain, decreasing to the wrist. The collar is ribbed
into the foundation chain, and the body is worked down from the split in the
second colour. The order below is the designer's: yoke, split, sleeves,
collar, body.

**Rounds.** Everything is worked in **joined, turned rounds**: every round
starts with ch 2 and a turn, and ends with a sl st into the top of its first
dc (under the V, not into the ch 2). **The ch 2 never counts as a stitch.**
The only rounds you don't turn before are the first round after attaching
yarn somewhere new.

**Corners.** From R2 on, the first dc of every yoke round goes into the
corner ch-1 space you just closed the previous round in. At the end of the
round that corner gets its second dc and its ch 1, and then the joining
sl st. So every corner ends up with (dc, ch 1, dc) each round; the first
one is just made in two halves.

**Colours.** A for the yoke, split, sleeves and collar; B for the body. The
yarn is attached fresh for each sleeve, for the collar and for the body:
slip knot on the hook, insert where the recipe says, pull a loop through,
tighten, then ch 2 and work the round without turning.

**dc2tog into a row end.** At each underarm the sleeve's first round has to
cross the side edge of the split round, where there are no stitch tops to
work into. The designer closes the gap with a dc2tog whose two legs go into
two gaps along that edge: start a dc into one gap, stop at 2 loops, start a
dc into the next gap, stop at 3 loops, yo and pull through all 3. Exactly
which gaps doesn't matter; the aim is a stitch that spans the edge without
leaving a hole.

**FPdc2tog.** A front-post decrease: yo, hook around the post of the first
stitch from the front, yo, pull up a loop, yo, pull through 2 (2 loops on
the hook); yo, around the post of the next stitch, yo, pull up, yo, pull
through 2 (3 loops); yo and pull through all 3. The collar uses it to merge
the two stitches either side of each corner space. A BPdc2tog is the same
with the hook going in from the back.

**Ribbing.** Rounds of [FPdc, BPdc], always starting with FPdc and ending
with BPdc, which is why the round before any ribbing must have an **even**
count. In later ribbing rounds, where the stitch below is a decrease it has
two posts: wrap both together and count them as one stitch.

**Fit checkpoints.** The neck chain sets the neck hole. The number of yoke
rounds sets the yoke depth and, with it, the width. The underarm chains add
width to body and sleeves without adding depth. The sleeve and body lengths
are up to you. Try it on at each of these points; the designer's numbers are
hers, not a size chart.

## Yoke (colour A)

**Start:** ch 100, or any multiple of 4 that fits comfortably over your
head with a little to spare (the ribbing closes the hole by a couple of cm;
100 ch is about 60 cm). Lay the chain out, make sure it isn't twisted, and
sl st into the first chain (under two loops) to make a ring.

**R1:** ch 2. [25 dc, one in each ch, then ch 1 for the corner] 4 times,
the first dc going into the same chain as the sl st. Don't miss the last
chain before the join. Sl st to the first dc. — 100 sts

If you chained a different number, each side is your chain ÷ 4.

**R2:** ch 2, turn. Dc into the corner ch-1 space you just made. *Dc in
each dc to the next corner, (dc, ch 1, dc) in the corner space;* repeat
from * at the next two corners, dc in each dc along the last side, then dc,
ch 1 into the first corner (the one you started in) to complete it. Sl st
to the first dc. — 108 sts

**R3–R15:** repeat R2. Each round adds 2 sts to every side. — 212 sts at R15

| ----- | -------- | ----- |
| 1     | 25       | 100   |
| 2     | 27       | 108   |
| 3     | 29       | 116   |
| 4     | 31       | 124   |
| 5     | 33       | 132   |
| 6     | 35       | 140   |
| 7     | 37       | 148   |
| 8     | 39       | 156   |
| 9     | 41       | 164   |
| 10    | 43       | 172   |
| 11    | 45       | 180   |
| 12    | 47       | 188   |
| 13    | 49       | 196   |
| 14    | 51       | 204   |
| 15    | 53       | 212   |

**Try it on** from about R12. Put your head through the hole and let the
square hang: its corners should reach a few cm below your underarms, which
is where the sleeves will start. Too high and the sleeves cut in; too low
and the underarm sags. The designer stopped at 15 rounds, 53 sts a side,
each side about 37 cm. Count all four sides to check they match.

## Splitting the yoke (colour A)

Decide the underarm chain first. Hold the two left corners of the square
together under your left arm and the two right corners under your right.
If body and arms fit comfortably like that, chain nothing at the underarms.
If you need more room, chain the extra width: each chain adds its length to
each sleeve, and twice its length to the body, because there are two of
them. Chain an even number, so a sleeve can start in its middle. The
designer chained 4.

**R16:** ch 2, turn. Dc into the corner ch-1 space, dc in each of the 53 dc
along the side, dc into the next corner space. Ch 4 (or your number). Skip
the whole next side; it becomes a sleeve. Dc into the corner space at the
far end of it, dc in each of the 53 dc along the side, dc into the next
corner space. Ch 4. Skip the last side. Sl st to the first dc. — 118 sts
(110 dc and 8 ch; the designer's written pattern gives 114, see Source)

The body opening is now a ring of two sides and two chains; each sleeve
opening is a ring of one side and one chain.

## Sleeves (colour A, make 2)

The first sleeve starts from the chain next to the join, which is the
chain the hook is nearest to. The sleeve opening should be above the chain
and the body opening below it once you've turned.

**R1:** turn. Sl st into the first 2 ch of the underarm chain (the two
nearest the hook); keep both slip stitches loose, they get worked into at
the end of the round. Ch 2, and don't turn again. Dc in each of the other
2 ch. Dc2tog into the gaps along the side edge of the split round (see
Before you start). Dc into the corner ch-1 space. Dc in each dc along the
sleeve side until you're at the **top of the shoulder**, halfway along
(fold the sleeve flat with the chain underneath; the fold line is the spot,
and it needn't be exact), dc2tog over the next 2 sts, then dc in each dc to
the end of the side. Dc into the corner space. Dc2tog into the gaps along
the other side edge of the split round; this one is where R16 began, so the
decrease goes around its ch 2. Dc into each of the 2 slip stitches. Sl st to
the first dc. — 60 sts

The designer's written pattern gives this round as 58 and the last plain
round as 40; counting what the instructions call for gives 60 and 42.
Either is fine, and the difference is two stitches around the arm. Count
your own R1 and carry on from that number; what matters is that the round
before the ribbing is even.

**R2:** ch 2, turn, dc in each st around, sl st to the first dc. — 60 sts

**R3:** ch 2, turn, dc in each st to the top of the shoulder, dc2tog over
the next 2 sts, dc in each st to the end, sl st. — 59 sts

**R4–R38:** repeat R2 and R3: a plain round, then a round with one decrease
at the top of the shoulder. Every odd round has 1 st fewer than the round
before it. Keep going until the sleeve reaches your wrist, or wherever you
want the cuff to start. Stop after a plain round. — 42 sts at R38

| Round          | Count |
| -------------- | ----- |
| 1 (decrease)   | 60    |
| 2              | 60    |
| 3 (decrease)   | 59    |
| 4              | 59    |
| 5 (decrease)   | 58    |
| …              | odd round 2k+1 has 60 − k |
| 37 (decrease)  | 42    |
| 38             | 42    |

Put every decrease at the top of the arm, not down by the chain. Decreasing
at the underarm bunches fabric there.

**R39–R41 (ribbing):** ch 2, turn, [FPdc around the next st, BPdc around
the next st] around, sl st to the first st. Where the stitch below is a
decrease, wrap both its posts as one stitch. With an even count the last
stitch is always a BPdc. — 42 sts

Three rounds is the designer's cuff; work more for a deeper one.

**Fasten off:** ch 1, cut the yarn leaving 10–15 cm, and pull it through.

**Second sleeve:** attach A to the other underarm chain, in its 2nd ch,
with the sweater turned so the sleeve will grow the same way as the first.
Ch 2 and work R1 from the "dc in each of the other 2 ch" on; at the end of
the round, dc into the 1st and 2nd ch, where the first sleeve had its slip
stitches (Ours: the designer says only to make it identical; she didn't
film it). Then R2–R41 as before. The seam of each sleeve sits on the
underside of the arm.

## Collar (colour A)

Worked into the foundation chain, around the posts of the yoke's R1. The
corner stitches either side of each ch-1 space are merged with a post-stitch
decrease, which takes the collar from 100 to 96 and stops the corners
flaring.

**Attach** A at the corner with the starting tail, in the dc to the right
of that corner's ch-1 space: put the hook under the loop of foundation
chain at the base of that dc and pull a loop through.

**R1:** ch 2. *FPdc2tog around the posts of the two dc either side of the
corner ch-1 space (at this first corner, the right-hand one is the yoke's
very first dc; wrap its ch 2 with it). Then [BPdc, FPdc] along the side,
stopping 1 st before the next corner space.* Repeat from * at the other
three corners, finishing with a BPdc. Sl st to the first st. — 96 sts

With 25 sts a side the 23 between decreases start and end on a BPdc, so
every corner decrease is a front-post one. If your sides have an even
count, the 2nd and 4th decreases become BPdc2tog instead.

**R2–R3:** ch 2, turn, [FPdc, BPdc] around, wrapping both posts of each
corner decrease as one stitch, sl st. — 96 sts

The designer did three rounds. Fasten off.

## Body (colour B)

**Attach** B in the middle of one underarm chain, on its underside: the
free loops the sleeve didn't use. Turn the sweater first so that the split
round's stitches face away from you, as if you'd chained 2 and turned; the
designer attaches "from the inside".

**R1:** ch 2, don't turn. Dc in every chain and every st of the split round
around, sl st to the first dc. — 118 sts (designer's written pattern: 114,
see Source)

**R2–R20:** ch 2, turn, dc in each st around, sl st. — 118 sts

The designer did 20 rounds, about 22 cm, with no shaping: the body falls
straight. Work to the length you want, stopping with an even count. For a
body that tapers in, see Variations.

**R21–R23 (ribbing):** ch 2, turn, [FPdc, BPdc] around, sl st. — 118 sts

Fasten off.

## Finishing

1. Tug each tail. If the stitches around it move, tie it off with a double
   knot before weaving; otherwise just weave.
2. Pull every tail through to the inside with the hook, then turn the
   sweater inside out.
3. Thread a tail on the needle and run it under the stitches of one row for
   a few cm, up to the next row, back the other way, then back along the
   first path again. Pull snug, not tight. Trim what's left. Repeat for
   every tail.
4. The designer doesn't block. Wool-Ease is mostly acrylic, so if you do,
   [steam it lightly](../../knowledge/techniques/garments.md#blocking-a-garment)
   rather than wet-blocking (Ours).

## Adjusting the size

The written pattern is one size, the designer's, and she sizes by trying on
rather than by a chart. Three things to change:

1. **The neck chain.** A multiple of 4 that goes over your head with a
   little room; the collar closes it a couple of cm. Each side of the square
   starts at chain ÷ 4.
2. **The yoke rounds.** Each adds 2 sts to every side and about 1.1 cm of
   depth. Stop when the corners sit a few cm below your underarms.
3. **The underarm chains.** Width without depth: each chain adds its length
   to each sleeve and twice that to the body.

For a sweater much bigger than hers, the designer estimates 1.5 times the
yarn. For a thinner yarn (DK), she says to chain more and work more rounds:
the foundation chain is what sets the neck hole.

**(Ours, untested)** The arithmetic, if you'd rather plan than try on, at
the designer's gauge (15 sts and 9 rounds to 10 cm) and with a 100 chain:

- stitches per side after R yoke rounds: 25 + 2 × (R − 1)
- body stitches: 2 × (side + 2) + 2 × underarm chain
- sleeve top: side + underarm chain + 3
- centimetres around ≈ stitches ÷ 1.5

| Chest, little ease | Yoke rounds | Per side | Underarm ch | Body sts | Sleeve top | Yoke depth |
| ------------------ | ----------- | -------- | ----------- | -------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| ~80 cm (as written)| 15          | 53       | 4           | 118 (79 cm) | 60 (40 cm) | 17 cm   |
| ~90 cm             | 17          | 57       | 8           | 134 (89 cm) | 68 (45 cm) | 19 cm   |
| ~100 cm            | 19          | 61       | 12          | 150 (100 cm)| 76 (51 cm) | 21 cm   |
| ~110 cm            | 21          | 65       | 16          | 166 (111 cm)| 84 (56 cm) | 23 cm   |

This is arithmetic, not a tested grading. The sleeve tops grow quickly, so
for the larger rows decrease the sleeve every round rather than every other
round, at least at first, and consider a slightly longer neck chain. Try it
on at the checkpoints regardless.

## Tips from the source

- **The ch 2 never counts.** Join each round with a sl st under the V of
  the first dc, not into the chain. If a round is one stitch off, that's
  the usual reason.
- **Don't miss the last stitch of a side** before a corner, and in R1 don't
  miss the last chain before the join; the gap it leaves is easy to spot.
- **Count the sides** of the square now and then; all four should match.
- **Keep the two underarm slip stitches loose**: you work dc into them at
  the end of the sleeve's first round.
- **Decrease at the top of the sleeve**, never at the underarm seam, or
  the underarm goes baggy.
- **Even count before ribbing.** If it's odd, add or skip one decrease. In
  ribbing, a decrease below counts as one stitch: wrap both posts.
- **Chain the neck a bit bigger** than you want the finished hole; the
  ribbing pulls it in.
- **Tails that move the stitches** get a double knot first; all tails go to
  the inside before weaving, and the weave changes direction so it can't
  work loose.

## Variations

From the designer:

- **Straight sleeves:** repeat R2 only, no decreases.
- **Faster taper:** decrease every round (repeat R3). **Slower:** two plain
  rounds between decreases.
- **Sleeve length:** stop early for a three-quarter sleeve, or go past the
  wrist for an extra-long one, then rib.
- **Tapered body:** alternate a plain round with a round that has one
  dc2tog at each side of the body, spacing the decrease rounds further apart
  for a gentler taper.
- **Deeper ribbing:** more rounds at any of the three edges.
- The same shape in stripes is her striped raglan pattern, linked from the
  written pattern page.

Ours, untested:

- **Three colours:** change colour at R16 so the sleeves differ from the
  yoke; or work the ribbing in the contrast colour.
- **One colour:** the structure is unchanged; you need about 390 g in all.
