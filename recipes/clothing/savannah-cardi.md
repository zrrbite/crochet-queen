# Savannah cardi

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9YrHOzYvCYY"><img src="https://img.youtube.com/vi/9YrHOzYvCYY/maxresdefault.jpg" alt="A cropped open-front cardigan in a variegated purple, blue and pink yarn with a lacy texture and ribbed cuffs and bands, worn over a white top; shown twice, closed at the front and slipped off one shoulder" width="480"></a>

*The video's thumbnail; click to watch.*

A cropped, open-front cardigan in Tunisian crochet: a back and two fronts
that are plain rectangles, two sleeves worked up from a ribbed cuff with a
few increases, all in a lacy two-row "arrowhead" stitch, seamed together and
finished with sideways ribbing along the hem and front edges. The designer
calls it beginner-friendly, and for someone who already does Tunisian
crochet it is, because nothing is shaped except the sleeves. We've marked it
intermediate: it needs a Tunisian hook (ideally a corded one), a different
way of working from ordinary crochet, a lace stitch built on yarn overs and
decreases, sleeve increases, and the seaming, ribbing and blocking of a full
garment.

## Sizing

Pick your size by the **largest** measurement around your torso, chest or
tummy, and add the 20–25 cm (8–10 in) of ease the design is built for. If
you'd rather have it less roomy, make a size down. The body and sleeve sizes
are independent: make the body in one size and the sleeves in another if
your upper arms or your sleeve length say so, and add or drop sleeve rows for
length (see [ease and sizing](../../knowledge/techniques/garments.md#ease-and-sizing)
and [measuring yourself](../../knowledge/techniques/garments.md#measuring-yourself)).

## Gauge

**16 sts × 11 rows = 12.5 cm (5 in)** in the arrowhead stitch with the 8 mm
hook, counting each yarn over as a stitch. Over 10 cm that's roughly 13 sts
× 9 rows (ours).

Gauge matters here: 10% off is a whole size. Make a
[gauge swatch](../../knowledge/techniques/garments.md#gauge-swatch) in the
arrowhead stitch and **wet-block it before measuring**; the designer found
this yarn's gauge drifts by about 2.5 cm (1 in) across a panel, and the
blocked cardigan is what has to fit.

## Before you start

**Construction.** Five flat pieces, then sewing
([construction](../../knowledge/techniques/garments.md#construction), the
"pieces" kind):

1. The **back** and **two fronts** are rectangles in the arrowhead stitch,
   worked from the hem up and bound off at the shoulder. No shaping.
2. The **sleeves** start with a strip of sc ribbing for the cuff, then the
   sleeve is worked up from the long edge of that strip, increasing at both
   edges every so often.
3. The fronts are seamed to the back across the shoulders; the sleeves are
   sewn flat into the armholes; the cardigan is blocked; then the underarm
   and side seams are closed.
4. Ribbing is crocheted straight onto the hem and then all round the front
   opening, attaching itself as it goes.

**Reading the sizes.** Numbers are given as XS (S, M, L, XL) (2XL, 3XL, 4XL,
5XL). Go through and highlight yours before you start; the designer does.

**How the rows work.**

- Every Tunisian row is a forward pass followed by the standard return pass
  (yo, pull through 1; then [yo, pull through 2] until one loop is left).
  "Return" below means exactly that.
- The right side faces you throughout; the work is never turned.
- The loop on the hook counts as the first stitch and the first vertical bar
  is skipped. The last stitch of every row is an **Lts**, worked under both
  strands of the edge stitch.
- The stitch count after a dash is the number of loops on the hook at the
  end of the forward pass. Count before every return pass.
- The arrowhead stitch is two rows: an **open row**, [tss2tog, yo] across,
  and a **closed row**, [tss 1, tks into the yo's hole] across. Row counts
  include the foundation row, which counts as a closed row, so odd rows are
  closed and even rows are open. All the body row counts are even: the
  panels end on an open row and the bind-off in pattern closes it.
- Chain loosely; it has to take a hook again, and later the ribbing.

**Abbreviations:** tss, tks, tfs, tss2tog, Lts and yo as on the
[Tunisian page](../../knowledge/techniques/tunisian-crochet.md#reading-a-tunisian-pattern);
sc, sl st, ch, BLO as usual.

## Back

**Foundation row:** with the 8 mm Tunisian hook, ch 60 (64, 74, 80, 86)
(94, 100, 106, 114). Pull up a loop in the back bump of the 2nd ch from the
hook and of every ch after it. Return. — 60 (64, 74, 80, 86) (94, 100, 106,
114) sts

**R1 (open):** [tss2tog, yo] across to the last st, Lts. Return. — 60 (64,
74, 80, 86) (94, 100, 106, 114) sts

**R2 (closed):** [tss 1, tks into the yo] across to the last st, Lts. Return.
— 60 (64, 74, 80, 86) (94, 100, 106, 114) sts

Repeat R1–2 another 14 (14, 16, 17, 18) (19, 20, 21, 23) times, then work R1
once more, for **32 (32, 36, 38, 40) (42, 44, 46, 50) rows in all**,
counting the foundation row. The last row is an open row. — 60 (64, 74, 80,
86) (94, 100, 106, 114) sts

At gauge the panel is now about 37 (37, 41.5, 44, 46) (48.5, 51, 53, 58) cm
tall before ribbing (ours, from the gauge). The panel width should match the
back width in the chart; if it's well off, stop and reconsider your hook.

**Bind off in pattern:** [tss the next st and pull it through the loop on
the hook; tks into the next yo and pull it through the loop on the hook]
across to the last st; Lts and pull it through. Keep every pull-through
loose. Fasten off, leaving a tail just long enough to weave in.

## Fronts (make 2)

Exactly as the back, narrower.

**Foundation row:** with the 8 mm Tunisian hook, ch 28 (30, 34, 38, 40)
(44, 48, 50, 54). Pull up a loop in the back bump of the 2nd ch from the hook
and of every ch after it. Return. — 28 (30, 34, 38, 40) (44, 48, 50, 54) sts

**R1 (open):** [tss2tog, yo] across to the last st, Lts. Return. — 28 (30,
34, 38, 40) (44, 48, 50, 54) sts

**R2 (closed):** [tss 1, tks into the yo] across to the last st, Lts. Return.
— 28 (30, 34, 38, 40) (44, 48, 50, 54) sts

Repeat R1–2 another 14 (14, 16, 17, 18) (19, 20, 21, 23) times, then R1 once
more: **32 (32, 36, 38, 40) (42, 44, 46, 50) rows**, the same as the back.
— 28 (30, 34, 38, 40) (44, 48, 50, 54) sts

**Bind off in pattern** as for the back. Fasten off leaving a tail **twice
the width of the panel**, plus a bit; it sews the shoulder seam.

Make the second front the same. The two fronts are identical; there's no
left and right until they're sewn on.

## Sleeves (make 2)

### Cuff ribbing (all sizes)

Worked sideways with the 6 mm regular hook, in rows of sc through the back
loop only. The rows run around the wrist; the ridges run up the arm.

**R1:** ch 7. Sc in the 2nd ch from the hook and in each ch across. Turn.
— 6 sc

**R2:** ch 1, sc BLO in each st across. Turn. — 6 sc

Repeat R2 until there are **24 (24, 24, 26, 26) (28, 28, 30, 30) rows**.
Don't fasten off and don't turn: rotate the strip to work along its long
edge, where the row ends are. Take the 6 mm hook out of the live loop and put
the 8 mm Tunisian hook in.

### Sleeve body

**Foundation row:** pull up a loop in the end of each ribbing row. The loop
already on the hook counts for the first row; for the rest, go into the base
of the end stitch of each row rather than into the knotty row end itself.
Return. — 24 (24, 24, 26, 26) (28, 28, 30, 30) sts

Count: the loops on the hook should equal the ribbing's rows.

From here the sleeve is the arrowhead stitch with increases at both edges
on certain rows. Five kinds of row occur:

- **Open:** [tss2tog, yo] across to the last st, Lts. Return. Same count.
- **Closed:** [tss 1, tks into the yo] across to the last st, Lts. Return.
  Same count.
- **Inc-open:** tfs in the first space (between the first and second
  stitches), [tss2tog, yo] across to the last st, tfs in the space just
  before the last st, Lts. Return. **+2 sts.**
- **Inc-closed:** tfs in the first space, tss 1, [tss 1, tks into the yo]
  across to the last 2 sts, tss 1, tfs in the space before the last st, Lts.
  Return. **+2 sts.** (The extra tss 1 at each end works the full stitch the
  row before added.)
- **Closed after an increase:** tss 1, [tss 1, tks into the yo] across to the
  last 2 sts, tss 1, Lts. Return. Same count. Only M and XL use it, as their
  final row.

Increases always come as an inc-open row followed by an inc-closed row, so
each pair adds 4 stitches and the pattern lines up again on the next open
row. The designer says working out how to increase the arrowhead stitch
cleanly took her a long time; don't improvise. Put a locking marker in the
first increase row so you can count repeats.

Follow your size.

### Sleeve bind-off (all sizes)

The sleeve's last row is a closed row, so a plain bind-off works: sl st
loosely in each st across (under the front bar, as for tss, then through
both loops on the hook) to the last st; Lts and pull through. Fasten off
leaving a tail **twice the width of the sleeve top** for sewing it in.

Make the second sleeve the same. If you marked the increases, take the
markers out now.

## Assembly

Have the tapestry needle, scissors and locking markers to hand. **Right side
out on every piece, arrows pointing up**; check each one before you sew.
Whip stitch or mattress stitch are both fine; the designer used mattress
stitch on her original and shows both in the video.

1. **Shoulders.** Lay the back flat, right side up. Lay a front on each side
   of it, right side up, with the bound-off edge of the front against the
   bound-off edge of the back and the outer corners matching. The whole top
   edge of each front is sewn to the back; the gap left between the two
   fronts in the middle is the back neck, only a few stitches wide (the
   front bands widen it later). Pin with markers, then sew each shoulder
   seam with the front's long tail.
   - *Mattress stitch*, right sides up: use only the inner loops of the
     bind-off V's on both pieces. Go in where you last came out and up
     through the next stitch, alternating sides; after a few stitches pull
     the yarn until the edges kiss. Finish by passing the needle under both
     loops on each side and through the loop it makes, to knot it.
   - *Whip stitch*, from the wrong side: under both loops of a stitch on one
     piece, up under both loops of the opposite stitch, repeat. Knot at the
     end the same way.
   Weave in the tails on the back.
2. **Sleeves.** Open the body out flat at the shoulders, right side up. Along
   the side edge, measure out your sleeve size's upper-arm width (15 (15,
   17, 17, 20) (20, 20, 21, 21) in; 38 (38, 43, 43, 51) (51, 51, 53.5,
   53.5) cm) centred on the shoulder seam, half on the back and half on the
   front, and mark both ends and the seam. Count the rows on each side of the
   seam to be sure they're equal. Mark the centre stitch of the sleeve top
   and match it to the shoulder seam. Sew the sleeve's bound-off edge to the
   body's row ends between the markers. Stitches and row ends don't match
   one to one, so now and then put two stitches into the same row end to
   keep the seam even; aim straight across. Repeat for the other sleeve.
3. **Block** now, with the body and sleeves flat. The designer strongly
   recommends it and wet-blocks: soak about 20 minutes, press the water out
   in a towel without wringing, pin out to the chart's measurements and let
   it dry. She pulls this yarn out quite hard. For an all-synthetic yarn,
   steam instead. It transforms the drape and keeps the seams flexible.
4. **Side seams.** Fold at the shoulders with right sides together. Cut a
   length of yarn two to three times the seam, knot it on at the cuff, and
   whip stitch from the cuff to the underarm and on down the side to the hem,
   stitch for stitch along the row ends. Take the last stitch right at the
   bottom edge so the ribbing has a clean corner to start from. Repeat on the
   other side.
5. Weave in all ends.

## Ribbing

Both bands are sc ribbing worked sideways with the 6 mm hook, through the
back loop only, and anchored to the cardigan with two slip stitches at the
end of every second row. Every pair of rows uses up two stitches (or row
ends) of the edge. The smaller hook keeps the band from flaring.

### Bottom band

Starts at the bottom corner of the **left** front, left as worn, and runs
along the hem into the foundation-chain V's that the back-bump foundation
left free.

**R1:** join with a sl st in the bottom corner stitch of the left front. Ch
9. Sc in the 2nd ch from the hook and in each ch across; sl st into the same
stitch you joined in, then sl st into the next stitch along the hem. Turn.
— 8 sc

**R2:** skip the 2 sl sts; sc BLO in each of the 8 sts (no ch 1). Turn.
— 8 sc

**R3:** ch 1, sc BLO in each of the 8 sts; sl st into the next 2 stitches
along the hem. Turn. — 8 sc

Repeat R2–3 along the whole hem, across the side seams and the back, to the
bottom corner of the right front. End with an R2, so the live loop is at the
outer edge, at the corner. **Don't fasten off.**

### Front band

Carries straight on from the bottom band: up the right front, across the
back neck and down the left front, attaching to row ends on the fronts and
to the neck stitches across the back.

**R1:** rotate the work to go up the right front edge. Ch 7. Sc in the 2nd
ch from the hook and in each ch across; sl st into the corner stitch and
into the next stitch up the front edge. Turn. — 6 sc

**R2:** skip the 2 sl sts; sc BLO in each of the 6 sts. Turn. — 6 sc

**R3:** ch 1, sc BLO in each of the 6 sts; sl st into the next 2 stitches up
the edge. Turn. — 6 sc

Repeat R2–3 all the way round the opening to the bottom corner of the left
front. Nothing special happens at the neck corners; just keep going. Where
the bottom band's edge meets the front edge it's lumpy; put a stitch in
wherever looks right and carry on. Fasten off.

## Finishing

Weave in all ends. Steam-block once more, going over the seams and the
ribbing, without stretching the ribbing. Then wear it.

## Tips from the source

- **Measure the biggest part of your torso**, chest or tummy, and choose by
  that. Get someone else to measure you. Highlight your size all through the
  pattern before you start.
- **Wet-block the swatch.** With this yarn the width can be 2.5 cm (1 in) off
  unblocked.
- **Loose chain, tall loops.** Every loop you pull up should reach the height
  of the row; that's what makes the lace open and the fabric drape.
- **Don't drop the yarn over** after the last tss2tog of a row; it's the
  stitch most often forgotten. Count loops before every return pass.
- **The last stitch** has a bar and a "shadow" behind it; get both, every
  row, or the edge goes loopy. Pull the stitch up and away from the row to
  see where the side of it is.
- **Count rows as open and closed**: foundation is closed, then they
  alternate. Finish the body panels on an open row so the bind-off in
  pattern lines up.
- **Count the ribbing stitches** now and then. It's surprisingly easy to lose
  one.
- **Mark the first increase row** on the sleeve with a locking marker; it
  makes counting repeats painless.
- **A long cord and no stopper**, the end tucked under your thigh, saves
  swapping cords between the narrow fronts and the wide back.
- **Setting in sleeves**, mark the centre of the sleeve and the ends of the
  armhole, and count rows each side; a sleeve sewn in a row off shows.
- **Block before the side seams**, while it lies flat.

## Variations

- **Longer:** add rows to the back and both fronts, keeping all three the
  same. The designer made a longer rainbow version and put buttonholes in
  the front band.
- **Pockets** on a longer version, the designer's suggestion.
- **Stripes**, or a lighter yarn; both hers.
- **Mixed sizes:** body in one size, sleeves in another, and sleeve length by
  adding or removing a row pair, all built into the pattern.
- The yarn is discontinued in some colours; the designer names Lion Brand
  Landscapes Renewed and Mandala Ombre as alternatives, or any worsted that
  meets gauge after blocking. A plain worsted will show the arrowheads more
  clearly than a variegated one (ours, untested).
