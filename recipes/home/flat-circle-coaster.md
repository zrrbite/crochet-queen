# Flat circle coaster

A solid, flat disc of double crochet in five joined rounds. Thick chunky yarn
on a small hook makes it dense enough to sit a mug on. A good first project in
the round: it builds up fast, and the designer made four or five in one
evening.

## Materials

- Chunky yarn, a small amount: well under one ball per coaster
- 5 mm hook
- Tapestry needle, scissors
- 1 stitch marker (optional, recommended)

5 mm is smaller than a chunky yarn's label usually suggests. That's on
purpose: it makes a firm, close fabric. With a thinner yarn, pick a hook at
the small end of its label range
(see [yarn and hooks](../../knowledge/materials/yarn-and-hooks.md)).

## Gauge

Not critical. Any size of circle works as a coaster as long as it lies flat.

## Before you start

- Worked in **joined rounds** with the right side facing you. Don't turn.
- The **ch 2 at the start of each round does not count as a stitch.** It's
  only there for height.
- So: the first dc of each round goes into the **same stitch the ch 2 comes
  out of**, and each round is joined with a sl st into the top of that
  **first dc**, never into the ch 2.
- Every round adds 10 stitches.

## Pattern

**Start:** make a magic ring.

**R1:** Ch 2, 10 dc into the ring. Pull the tail to close the centre tight.
Sl st into the first dc to join. — 10 sts

**R2:** Ch 2, 2 dc in each st around. Sl st into the first dc to join.
— 20 sts

**R3:** Ch 2, \*1 dc in next st, 2 dc in next st\*; rep from \* around.
Sl st into the first dc to join. — 30 sts

**R4:** Ch 2, \*1 dc in each of next 2 sts, 2 dc in next st\*; rep from \*
around. Sl st into the first dc to join. — 40 sts

**R5:** Ch 2, \*1 dc in each of next 3 sts, 2 dc in next st\*; rep from \*
around. Join with a sl st, or for a neater edge, with an invisible join.
— 50 sts

At a glance:

| Round | Repeat 10 times     | Stitches |
| ----- | ------------------- | -------- |
| 1     | 1 dc into ring      | 10       |
| 2     | 2 dc in next        | 20       |
| 3     | 1 dc, 2 dc in next  | 30       |
| 4     | 2 dc, 2 dc in next  | 40       |
| 5     | 3 dc, 2 dc in next  | 50       |

## Finishing

1. Cut the yarn, leaving about 15 cm (6 in), and pull it through the loop.
2. If you didn't sl st join R5, finish with an invisible join. The designer
   does this, threading the tail under the top loops of the first stitch so
   the edge looks unbroken.
3. Turn the coaster over and weave the end in towards the centre along the
   back.
4. Coasters get washed a lot, so before trimming, tie two or three small knots
   in the tail at the centre of the back. The bump won't show. A dot of fabric
   glue works too.
5. Weave in the starting tail the same way, then trim both.
6. If it domes a little, spray-block it flat
   ([how](../../knowledge/techniques/finishing.md#blocking)).

## Tips from the source

- **Count before every join.** The designer's warning: get distracted
  mid-round, skip an increase, and you're making a cone, not a coaster.
- **Use a stitch marker anyway.** She says that however long you've been
  crocheting, assuming you don't need one is a mistake.
- **Closing the seam on R2.** She works only 1 dc into the first stitch at the
  start of the round and comes back for the second dc of that increase at the
  very end, just before the join. Same count, and the seam fills in instead of
  gapping.
- **Gap on the last round.** If the final join still leaves a gap, she adds
  one extra dc before joining. Only do this on the last round: on any earlier
  round it throws the next round's repeat off by one.
- **Doming is normal.** Yarn, hook, fibre, and tension creeping between rounds
  all make a new circle sit a little domed. Block it rather than unpicking.

## Variations

- **Bigger:** keep going with one more plain dc before each increase per round.
  R6: \*4 dc, 2 dc in next\* — 60 sts; R7: \*5 dc, 2 dc in next\* — 70 sts.
  Large ones make plant-pot mats. For a trivet under hot pans, use cotton, not
  acrylic.
- **A set in graduated sizes**, stacked or laid together as a centrepiece.
- **Colour change** every round or two for stripes.
- **An edging**, such as a round of sl st or reverse sc, for a firmer rim.
