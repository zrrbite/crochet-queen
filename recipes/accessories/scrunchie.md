# Scrunchie

A ruffled scrunchie crocheted straight onto an ordinary elastic hair tie:
one round of single crochet hides the elastic, two rounds of double crochet
make the ruffle, and an optional single-crochet round in a second colour
edges it. Quick to make, and a good way to use up leftover yarn.

## Materials

- Worsted-weight yarn: a main colour, plus a contrast colour for the edge
  (optional)
- 5 mm hook
- 1 elastic hair tie (the plain fabric-covered kind)
- Tapestry needle, scissors

## Gauge

Not needed.

## Stitches and techniques

- [Slip knot, chain, slip stitch, single crochet, double crochet](../../knowledge/stitches/basic-stitches.md)
- [Changing colour](../../knowledge/techniques/colour-changes.md)

## Before you start

- Worked in **joined rounds** around the hair tie, right side facing. Don't
  turn.
- There's no fixed stitch count: R1 is however many sc it takes to cover your
  hair tie, and each later round follows from that.
- In R2 and R3 the **ch 3 counts as the first dc**.

## Pattern

**Attach:** make a slip knot. Push the knot through the hair tie, put the hook
back into the knot, and pull the yarn through the loop. The yarn is now
fastened to the band.

**R1:** sc around the hair tie: insert the hook through the middle of the
band, yo, pull up a loop, yo, pull through both. Keep going, sliding the
stitches together as you work, until the elastic is completely hidden.
Sl st into the first sc to join.

How many you fit here sets the fullness: more sc, more ruffle.

**R2:** ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 2 dc in the same st. 3 dc in each sc around.
Sl st into the top of the ch 3 to join. — 3 × the R1 count

The stitches get crowded; check that each new one goes into the next sc, not
one hidden under the last group.

**R3:** ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 1 dc in the same st. \*1 dc in next st, 2 dc in
next st\*; rep from \* around. Join with a sl st, or go straight to R4 for a
colour edge.

For a fuller ruffle, work 2 dc or even 3 dc in every stitch of R3.

**R4 (optional colour edge):** cut the main colour, leaving a tail. Insert the
hook into the first stitch of R3 and pull the new colour through as a sl st;
that both joins R3 and starts the new colour. Tie the two tails together.
Ch 1, sc in the same st, then sc in each st around, working over the tails to
hide them. Sl st into the first sc to join.

**Fasten off:** ch 1, cut the yarn, and pull the tail through. Weave in the
ends, including any left from R1.

## Tips from the source

- More stitches anywhere means more ruffle: pack more into R1, or put more dc
  per stitch in R3.
- Scrunchies make easy gifts. Make a few in matching colours.

## Variations

- Skip R4 for a single-colour scrunchie.
- *(Ours)* A soft cotton or a velvet yarn changes the look entirely; velvet
  makes a very plush scrunchie.
