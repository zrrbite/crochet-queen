# Bucket hat

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I6m_woyNvZk"><img src="https://img.youtube.com/vi/I6m_woyNvZk/maxresdefault.jpg" alt="A cream crocheted bucket hat, held up and worn" width="480"></a>

*The video's thumbnail; click to watch.*

A simple bucket hat in double crochet: a flat circle for the crown, straight
rounds for the sides, and a brim that flares out because it increases more
slowly than the crown did. Every part is sized by measuring as you go, so it
adapts to any head, yarn or hook. The designer wrote it as a first project.

## Materials

- About 70 g of worsted-weight yarn
- 5.5 mm hook (whatever your yarn's label recommends)
- Tapestry needle, scissors
- Tape measure (optional but handy)

## Gauge

13 dc × 8.5 rounds = 10 × 10 cm.

You don't need to match it, because the pattern sizes by measuring: the
crown is worked until it's a set **diameter**, the sides until they reach your
**eyebrows**.

## Stitches and techniques

- [Chain, slip stitch, double crochet](../../knowledge/stitches/basic-stitches.md)
- [Flat circles](../../knowledge/techniques/flat-circles.md): the crown is
  one, with 12 starting stitches
- [Magic ring](../../knowledge/techniques/magic-ring.md), or the chain ring
  the designer uses
- [Weaving in ends](../../knowledge/techniques/finishing.md#weaving-in-ends)

## Before you start

- Worked in **joined rounds**, right side facing. Don't turn.
- **Every round starts with ch 2, which does not count as a stitch.** The
  first dc goes into the same stitch as the join, at the base of the ch 2.
- **Every round ends with a sl st** into the top of its first dc (under the V
  of that stitch, not into the ch 2). Don't count the sl st as a stitch.

## Crown

**Start:** ch 4 and sl st into the first ch to make a ring, or make a magic
ring.

**R1:** ch 2, 12 dc into the ring. Pull the tail to tighten the centre.
Sl st to join. — 12 sts

**R2:** ch 2, 2 dc in each st around. Join. — 24 sts

**R3:** ch 2, [1 dc, 2 dc in next st] 12 times. Join. — 36 sts

**R4:** ch 2, [2 dc, 2 dc in next st] 12 times. Join. — 48 sts

**R5:** ch 2, [3 dc, 2 dc in next st] 12 times. Join. — 60 sts

**R6:** ch 2, [4 dc, 2 dc in next st] 12 times. Join. — 72 sts

**Measure.** Lay the crown flat and measure across:

| Size         | Crown diameter | Rounds (at the designer's gauge) |
| ------------ | -------------- | -------------------------------- |
| small/medium | 16 cm          | 6 (72 sts)                       |
| medium/large | 18 cm          | 7 (84 sts)                       |

For a bigger crown, keep adding one more dc before each increase:
R7: [5 dc, 2 dc in next st] 12 times — 84 sts. Stop when you reach the
diameter, whatever round that is.

## Sides

**Next rounds:** ch 2, 1 dc in each st around. Join. — same count as the last
crown round

Repeat until the hat reaches about 2–3 cm (an inch) above your eyebrows when
you try it on. The designer's small/medium took **7 rounds**, about 8 cm.

## Brim

The brim increases in **6 places** per round, half as often as the crown did,
so it angles outwards instead of lying flat.

**Find your starting repeat:** stitch count ÷ 6 = how often to increase.
For 72 stitches, that's every 12th stitch: 11 dc, then 2 dc in the 12th.

**Brim R1:** ch 2, [11 dc, 2 dc in next st] 6 times. Join. — 78 sts

**Brim R2:** ch 2, [12 dc, 2 dc in next st] 6 times. Join. — 84 sts

**Brim R3:** ch 2, [13 dc, 2 dc in next st] 6 times. Join. — 90 sts

**Brim R4:** ch 2, [14 dc, 2 dc in next st] 6 times. Join. — 96 sts

Each round adds one dc before the increase and 6 stitches in all. Four rounds
make a brim about 5 cm wide; add or drop rounds for a wider or narrower one.

For a medium/large (84 sts): 84 ÷ 6 = 14, so brim R1 is [13 dc, 2 dc in next
st] 6 times — 90 sts, and so on.

## Finishing

1. Ch 1, cut the yarn, leaving about 15 cm, and pull it through the loop.
2. Turn the hat inside out. Weave the tail under the loops at the base of the
   stitches along one round, then up a round and back the other way. Changing
   direction keeps it from working loose. Keep it loose enough that the
   fabric doesn't pucker.
3. Do the same with the starting tail at the centre of the crown.

## Tips from the source

- **Count each round's total**, ignoring the ch 2 and the sl st. If the count
  is right and you've followed the repeat, the last stitch of an increase
  round is always an increase.
- **Crowded first round:** if 12 dc won't fit in the ring, slide them round
  to make room.
- **Count stitches by their posts or by the V's on top**, whichever is easier
  to see in your yarn.
