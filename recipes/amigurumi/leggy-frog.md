# Leggy frog (no sew)

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IM2LK_vEgcA"><img src="https://img.youtube.com/vi/IM2LK_vEgcA/maxresdefault.jpg" alt="A lime-green crocheted frog with long dangling legs" width="480"></a>

*The video's thumbnail; click to watch.*

A squishy frog in chunky blanket yarn with long dangly arms and legs, each
ending in three little loop fingers. The limbs are crocheted straight off the
body as you go, and the bottom is crocheted shut, so there's nothing to sew.
Quick: one sitting.

## Materials

- Super bulky blanket yarn (chenille-style, like Bernat Blanket) or velvet
  yarn: well under one ball
- Scraps of pink (cheeks) and black or dark green (mouth)
- 6 mm hook
- 2 × 10 mm safety eyes
- A handful of fibre fill
- 1 stitch marker, scissors

No tapestry needle needed: the designer pulls every yarn end through with the
hook.

## Gauge

Not critical.

## Stitches and techniques

- [Chain, single crochet, half double crochet, slip stitch](../../knowledge/stitches/basic-stitches.md)
- [Single crochet decrease (sc2tog), increase](../../knowledge/stitches/increases-and-decreases.md)
- [Amigurumi](../../knowledge/techniques/amigurumi.md): spiral rounds,
  starting an oval from a chain, safety eyes, embroidering a face

## Before you start

- **Continuous rounds** (a spiral). Mark the first stitch of each round.
- The frog is worked from the **top of the head down**. The start oval is the
  head; eyes go near the top.
- Blanket yarn hides its stitches, so count by feel as much as by eye. Plenty
  of stitch markers help.

## The limb

Worked the same way four times, straight out from the body:

> **Limb:** ch 14. Sc in the 4th ch from the hook (the 3 chains you skip make
> the first finger loop). [Ch 3, sc in that same ch] 2 times, for three
> fingers in all. Then 1 sc in each of the 10 remaining chains, back up to the
> body.

## Pattern

**Start:** ch 9. Put a marker in the 9th chain, the one just below the hook.

**R1:** 2 hdc in the 3rd ch from the hook. The 2 chains you skipped count as
1 hdc, and the marker marks their top. 1 sc in each of the next 5 ch, 4 hdc in
the last ch. Rotate to work along the other side of the chain: 1 sc in each of
the next 5 ch, 1 hdc in the last ch (the one holding the first 2 hdc).
— 18 sts

Each end of the oval now has 4 hdc's worth of stitches, and each side 5 sc.
The marked ch-2 is the first stitch of R2.

**R2:** sc2tog, 7 sc, sc2tog, 7 sc. — 16 sts

**R3–4:** sc in each st around (2 rounds). — 16 sts

**Face:**

- **Eyes:** between R1 and R2, one on each side of the head, symmetrical.
  Press the washers on firmly from the inside.
- **Cheeks:** a short piece of pink yarn, pulled in and out with the hook one
  stitch wide, about one stitch below each eye. Tie the ends together on the
  inside.
- **Mouth:** black or dark green, between R2 and R3, 3 stitches wide, tied on
  the inside. Catch the middle down for a V-shaped smile if you like.

**R5:** 8 sc, plus 1 or 2 more if needed to reach the side of the frog. Work a
limb (arm). 8 sc along the body, to the other side. Work a limb (arm).

**R6:** sc in each st around, working only into the body. Push each arm
outwards out of the way to find the next body stitch. — 16 sts

**R7:** [7 sc, inc] 2 times. — 18 sts

**R8–11:** sc in each st around (4 rounds). — 18 sts

**R12:** sc a few more stitches until you're at the side of the frog. Work a
limb (first leg).

**Close the bottom:** fold the frog flat so the open edge lines up front to
back. Starting next to the leg you just made, insert the hook through a
stitch on the front and the stitch directly behind it, and sl st them
together. Do this for 3 pairs.

**Stuff** now, through the gap that's left. A handful, pushed in half at a
time. The designer keeps hers soft and squishy, not firm.

Continue the sl st join for 4 more pairs (7 in all), to the other side. Work a
limb (second leg). Sl st the last pair together to close the frog.

**Finish:** cut the yarn. With the hook, pull the end into the belly and out
again a couple of times, then trim. Blanket yarn grips itself, so this holds
without weaving in.

## Tips from the source

- **Add stitches to reach the side** before each limb (R5 and R12). Exactly how
  many depends on your tension; the point is that arms and legs sit on the
  frog's sides.
- **Round 6 is the fiddly one:** the arms get in the way. Fold each one
  outwards and work only into the body stitches.
- **The fingers are hollow loops**, so you can thread one hand through the
  other and hang the frog on a bag strap or a zip.
- Skip the cheeks, move the mouth, make it crooked. Every frog can be a bit
  different.
