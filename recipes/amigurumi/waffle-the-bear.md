# Waffle the bear

A chubby little bear whose head and body are one oval piece, worked in a
spiral and stuffed. Add two ears, two arms and a white muzzle, then
embroider a nose. Only four small parts to sew on, so it makes a good
second amigurumi.

|              |                                                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Type         | amigurumi                                              |
| Difficulty   | easy: the designer suggests one amigurumi first        |
| Terms        | US (source used: US)                                   |
| Yarn         | sport weight (CYC 2), a main colour and white. Designer used WeCrochet Brava Sport |
| Hook         | 3.25 mm (US D-3)                                       |
| Finished size| about 9 × 6 cm (3.5 × 2.5 in), per the designer        |
| Source       | [Easy Beginner Crochet Teddy Bear Tutorial: Waffle the Bear](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8l630vFgbE), by Little World of Whimsy |

## Materials

- Sport-weight yarn: main colour (body, ears, arms), plus white for the muzzle.
  Well under a ball of each.
- 3.25 mm hook
- 2 × 6 mm safety eyes, or black yarn to embroider eyes (see the safety note)
- Black embroidery thread for the nose
- Polyester fibre fill (toy stuffing)
- Tapestry needle (a bent-tip one helps when sewing parts on), scissors,
  stitch markers, sewing pins

Any yarn works with a hook a size or two smaller than its label suggests; the
bear just comes out bigger or smaller. Thicker yarn makes a bigger bear.

## Gauge

Not critical, but the fabric must be **tight enough that stuffing doesn't show
through**. If you can see fibre fill between stitches, go down a hook size.

## Stitches and techniques

- [Single crochet, slip stitch](../../knowledge/stitches/basic-stitches.md)
- [Increases and decreases](../../knowledge/stitches/increases-and-decreases.md),
  including the invisible decrease
- [Amigurumi](../../knowledge/techniques/amigurumi.md): spiral rounds,
  starting an oval from a chain, turning right side out, stuffing, safety eyes,
  closing, sewing parts on, embroidering a face
- [Magic ring](../../knowledge/techniques/magic-ring.md)

## Before you start

- Everything is worked in **continuous rounds** (a spiral): no joins, no
  starting chains. Put a stitch marker in the last stitch of each round and
  move it up as you go.
- To find the first stitch of a new round, count back from the marker.
- `inc` = 2 sc in the same stitch. `dec` = invisible decrease, unless a round
  says otherwise.

## Head and body (main colour)

The body starts as an oval worked around both sides of a short chain, so the
bear ends up wider than it is deep.

**R1:** Ch 7. Starting in the 2nd ch from the hook, 1 sc in each of the next
5 ch, 2 sc in the last ch. Rotate the work so you can crochet along the other
side of the chain, and 1 sc in each of the 5 ch on that side. — 12 sts

From here on it's ordinary rounds.

**R2:** inc in each st around. — 24 sts

**R3:** [3 sc, inc] 6 times. — 30 sts

**R4:** [4 sc, inc] 6 times. — 36 sts

**R5:** [5 sc, inc] 6 times. — 42 sts

**R6:** [6 sc, inc] 6 times. — 48 sts

It may look a bit hexagonal now. That smooths out once the increases stop.

**R7–10:** sc in each st around (4 rounds). — 48 sts

The piece is turning into a bowl. **Turn it right side out** now: the side
with the V-shaped stitches goes outside, the side with little horizontal bars
inside.

**R11:** [7 sc, inc] 6 times. — 54 sts

**Eyes:** put the safety eyes in now, but leave the washers off until you're
sure of the spot. Place them **between R9 and R10**, counting from the top
(R1 is the chain). Put them on one of the two long sides of the oval, not the
narrow ends, with **7 stitches between them**. Once you're happy, push the
washers on from the inside.

**R12–19:** sc in each st around (8 rounds). — 54 sts

**R20:** [8 sc, inc] 6 times. — 60 sts

**R21–25:** sc in each st around (5 rounds). — 60 sts

Now the base, decreasing:

**R26:** [8 sc, dec] 6 times. — 54 sts

**R27:** [7 sc, dec] 6 times. — 48 sts

**R28:** [4 sc, dec] 8 times. — 40 sts

Note the switch from 6 repeats to 8. More decreases per round pull the base
in faster.

**R29:** [3 sc, dec] 8 times. — 32 sts

**R30:** [2 sc, dec] 8 times. — 24 sts

**Stuff** now. Pull the fibre fill into small tufts and layer them in from the
bottom up, so there are no lumps. Stuff firmly: when you press it, it should
bounce back.

**R31:** [1 sc, dec] 8 times. — 16 sts

Last chance to top up the stuffing; fill it right up.

**R32:** dec 8 times. — 8 sts

The hole is small now and front loops are hard to reach, so the designer
switches to an ordinary sc2tog for the last two rounds.

**R33:** dec 4 times. — 4 sts

**Close:** cut the yarn, leaving 20–25 cm (8–10 in). Thread it on the needle,
weave over and under through the last round's stitches, and pull tight to
cinch the hole shut. Push the needle down through the centre and out anywhere
on the body, then cut the yarn flush so the end disappears inside.

## Arms (main colour, make 2)

**R1:** 6 sc in a magic ring, pull closed. — 6 sts

**R2:** sc in each st around. — 6 sts

Turn this tiny tube right side out now. It only gets harder later.

**R3–4:** sc in each st around. — 6 sts

Fasten off, leaving about 25 cm (10 in) for sewing. Fold the arm flat. Tuck
the starting tail inside.

## Ears (main colour, make 2)

**R1:** 6 sc in a magic ring, pull closed. — 6 sts

**R2:** inc in each st around. — 12 sts

**R3–4:** sc in each st around, turning the piece right side out as you go.
— 12 sts

Fasten off with a 25 cm (10 in) tail. Fold flat; trim the starting tail on the
inside.

## Muzzle (white)

**R1:** 6 sc in a magic ring, pull closed. — 6 sts

**R2:** [1 sc, inc] 3 times. — 9 sts

**R3:** [2 sc, inc] 3 times. — 12 sts

**R4:** sc in each st around, turning it right side out as you go. — 12 sts

Sl st into the next stitch and fasten off with a 25 cm (10 in) tail.

**Nose:** with black embroidery thread, stitch a small filled triangle in the
centre of the muzzle, point down.

Instead of cutting the muzzle's starting tail, ball it up inside. That's all
the stuffing the muzzle needs.

## Assembly

Pin every piece first and look at the bear before sewing anything. Placement
by rounds, counting from the top of the body:

| Piece  | Where                                                         |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Muzzle | centred between the eyes, its top level with the top of the eyes; spans about R9–R15 |
| Ears   | either side of the top, between R3 and R8                     |
| Arms   | between R14 and R16, just below the muzzle, one each side     |

**Ears:** whip stitch: needle under a stitch of the body, then up through the
bottom row of the ear, all the way across. Keep the ear's curve as you go.

**Muzzle:** the same, catching one stitch of the muzzle's edge each time.
It's small and you'll skip stitches, so go round twice if it feels loose.

**Arms:** in through the body just beside the arm, out through the arm, back
into the body at the next stitch. Go back over it if it isn't secure.

After each piece, push the needle into the body and out somewhere else to
bury the tail, then cut it flush. Push any ends that still show back in with
the needle or hook.

## Tips from the source

- **Safety eyes are not safe for small children**: they can come out and be
  swallowed. For a bear going to a baby or toddler, embroider the eyes with
  black yarn or use felt.
- **Turn right side out early**, around R10 on the body and R2 on the arms.
  Small pieces get hard to turn later.
- **Stuffing showing through** means your stitches are too loose: tighten up or
  go down a hook size.
- Dress it up: a little hat, or a heart for it to hold
  (see [the heart](../appliques/mini-heart.md)).

## Variations

The designer's written pattern has since been updated for a fluffy bulky yarn
on a 2.75 mm hook, which gives a bigger bear, about 10 × 15 cm (4 × 6 in), with
an 18-stitch muzzle worked in joined rounds. The body is the same.
