# No-sew dragon

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LF8BHi7Zaes"><img src="https://img.youtube.com/vi/LF8BHi7Zaes/maxresdefault.jpg" alt="A pale sage-green crocheted dragon lying on white fur, with dark green scalloped wings, white horns and a ridge of spikes down its back" width="480"></a>

*The video's thumbnail; click to watch.*

A lying-down dragon in chenille yarn. Head, body and tail are one piece,
worked from the tip of the nose to the tip of the tail; the wings, four feet,
ears and horns are made first and crocheted into the body as the rounds go
past, so nothing is sewn on. The needle only comes out for toenails,
nostrils and eye details, and the spikes are surface crocheted down the back
at the end. It is a clear step up from the bear and the frog: ten separate
parts, each of which has to be made, closed flat, and then caught into the
right stitches of the right round facing the right way; several asymmetric
rounds whose start has to be kept centred under the belly; and chenille
yarn, which is lovely to squish but hides the stitches, so you count by feel.

## Materials

- Super bulky chenille yarn in three colours: main (head, body, feet, ears,
  wing borders), accent (wings, toenails, nostrils, spikes) and white (horns,
  eye highlights). Amounts not given; the main colour needs by far the most
  (Ours, estimate: one large skein of it, much less of the other two).
- 5 mm hook. A size smaller helps for the horns if the tiny rounds are hard.
- 2 × 20 mm safety eyes (see the safety note under Tips)
- Polyester fibre fill
- Stitch markers: at least three; the head has three in at once
- Tapestry or embroidery needle, scissors
- Sewing pins (optional) to hold a part in place while you crochet it on

## Gauge

Not critical. Chenille on a 5 mm hook makes a dense fabric and the stuffing
won't show. Looser or tighter stitches only change the finished size and how
many "shift" stitches you need (see below).

## Before you start

**How it goes together.** Make the parts first: 2 wings, 4 feet, 2 ears,
2 horns. Then the head, from the nose backwards; ears and horns are caught
into R11, the eyes go in after R14. The body carries straight on from the head
without cutting the yarn: wings in R18, front feet in R21, back feet in R26,
then the tail narrows to a point and is closed. Last come the embroidered
face and the spikes, surface crocheted from between the horns to the tip of
the tail. The designer numbers head, body and tail in one run, R1–R47, and so
does this.

**Rounds and rows.** Everything round is worked in continuous rounds (a
spiral). The designer keeps her marker in the **last** stitch of each round;
do the same so the counts line up with hers. The wing border and the ears are
worked in rows, turned with a ch 1 or ch 3 that doesn't count as a stitch.

**Counts.** `inc` = 2 sc in one stitch. `dec` = a single crochet decrease;
the designer doesn't say which kind, and in chenille either is invisible.
`— n sts` is the count at the end of the round or row. A stitch worked
through a part and the body together counts as one body stitch.

**Closing a part flat.** Feet, horns and wings are closed with the hook, not
a needle. Flatten the opening so the two halves line up. Insert the hook
through a stitch on the near layer and the stitch directly behind it on the
far layer, and make a sc. Repeat across: n sc close 2n stitches and leave a
neat edge of n stitches to attach the part by.

**Crocheting a part on.** Hold the finished part against the outside of the
body with its closed edge along the next few stitches of the round. Insert
the hook through a stitch of the part's edge and then through the next body
stitch, pull up a loop and finish the sc as normal. Chenille hides the part's
stitches, so go in wherever the hook will go, spaced evenly along the edge.
Each of these sc is one stitch of the round; the counts don't change. A
foot's 6-stitch edge is attached with 6 sc, an ear's 5 with 5, a horn's 3
with 3, and a wing by its corner with 3.

**Shift stitches.** Several rounds only work if they start at the centre of
the underside (the chin, later the belly), so that increases and parts land
symmetrically. A spiral drifts, so before those rounds the designer works a
few extra plain sc past the marker until the hook is at the centre of the
underside, then moves the marker and starts the round there. How many you
need depends on your tension; she needed two or three each time. They don't
change the stitch count.

**Surface crochet.** Stitches worked on top of a finished fabric. Hold the
yarn behind the fabric and the hook in front. Insert the hook through the
fabric, catch the yarn and pull a loop up to the front. For each further
stitch, insert the hook through the fabric a stitch further along, instead
of into a stitch of a row, pull up a loop and finish the stitch as usual: for
a sc, yarn over and pull through both loops; for a dc, yarn over before
inserting and work the loops off two at a time; for a sl st, pull the new
loop straight through the loop on the hook. The stitches sit on the surface
like a raised line of embroidery.

**Wrapped embroidery.** The toenails and nostrils are short bars of yarn
built up by wrapping: bring the needle up at one point, down two or three
stitches away, up again at the first point, and repeat through the same two
holes three or four times until the wraps stand proud. To get to the next
bar, travel under the surface and come up where it starts.

**Chenille.** Thick, slippery, and it hides the stitches. Count by feel, mark
anything you'll need to find again (the front of each foot, the centre of the
face), and pull hard when you tie ends together: chenille knots hold, but
only when tight.

## Wings (make 2)

Start in the accent colour.

**R1:** 6 sc in a magic ring, pull closed. — 6 sts

**R2:** inc in each st around. — 12 sts

**R3:** [1 sc, inc] 6 times. — 18 sts

**R4:** [2 sc, inc] 6 times. — 24 sts

**R5:** [3 sc, inc] 6 times. — 30 sts

**R6:** [4 sc, inc] 6 times. — 36 sts

Fold the circle in half with the wrong side (the side with the magic-ring
tail) inside. From here on you work in rows along the curved edge, through
both layers.

**Row 7:** 18 sc through both layers, from one end of the fold to the other.
Change to the main colour on the last step of the 18th sc. — 18 sts

**Row 8:** ch 1, turn. 18 sc. — 18 sts

**Row 9:** ch 1, turn. [3 dc in the next st, sl st in each of the next 2 sts]
6 times. — 30 sts, 6 scallops

Fasten off. Tie the two ends from the colour change together, then push
every end in between the layers of the wing.

**Webbing:** two lines of surface crochet in the main colour, splitting the
half-circle into three. Insert the hook at the scalloped edge by the second
scallop in from one end, pull up a loop and make a sc. Surface crochet in sc
in a straight line to the middle of the folded edge (the centre of the magic
ring), then straight back out to the scalloped edge by the second scallop
from the other end, so the two lines meet in a point at the fold. Finding a
spot for the hook in chenille takes patience; just keep the lines straight.
Fasten off and pull the ends in between the layers.

## Feet (make 4)

Main colour.

**R1:** 6 sc in a magic ring, pull closed. — 6 sts

**R2:** inc in each st around. — 12 sts

**R3:** 3 sc, inc 6 times, 3 sc. — 18 sts

The six increases are the front of the foot. Mark the first and last of them
if you don't trust yourself to find them again; you need them when closing.

**R4–5:** sc in each st around (2 rounds). — 18 sts

**R6:** 3 sc, dec 6 times, 3 sc. — 12 sts

Stuff. Now close it so that the six front stitches (the decreases) are one
side of the seam and the six back stitches the other: work shift stitches
(the designer needed 3) until the hook is exactly where the front stitches
begin, fold the opening flat at that point, and sc 6 through both layers.
Seen from above, the seam runs across the foot with the rounded front on one
side of it and the flatter back on the other. — 6 sts

Fasten off; no tail needed.

**Toenails:** thread the accent colour on the needle, leaving a tail
hanging where you first go in. On the front face of the foot make four
wrapped bars side by side, each standing about three rounds tall, three or
four wraps thick, a stitch or two apart: wrap one, travel under the surface
to the top of the next, wrap straight down from there. Finish by coming out
next to the starting tail; tie the two ends together and bury them in the
foot.

## Ears (make 2)

Main colour. Three fans of 5 sc, each worked into one chain, stepping up like
a staircase; then a straight edge along the bottom to attach by.

**Ch 6.**

**Row 1:** 5 sc in the 2nd ch from the hook, all five in that one chain. The
remaining 4 chains stay unworked for now. — 5 sts

**Row 2:** ch 1, turn. 1 sc in each of the first 3 sc. — 3 sts

**Row 3:** ch 3, turn. 5 sc in the 2nd ch from the hook. — 5 sts

**Row 4:** ch 1, turn. 1 sc in each of the first 3 sc. — 3 sts

**Row 5:** ch 3, turn. 5 sc in the 2nd ch from the hook. — 5 sts

**Bottom edge:** hold the piece with the three fans at the top, wiggly edge
up, and work 5 sc along the bottom edge, back towards the starting chain,
going into the row ends and the leftover chain. This straight edge is what
gets crocheted onto the head. — 5 sts

Fasten off; no tail needed.

## Horns (make 2)

White. The rounds are tiny; go down a hook size if it helps.

**R1:** 4 sc in a magic ring, pull closed. — 4 sts

**R2–3:** sc in each st around (2 rounds). — 4 sts

**R4:** [1 sc, inc] 2 times. — 6 sts

**R5:** inc 2 times, dec 2 times. — 6 sts

That round is what bends the horn.

**R6:** sc in each st around. — 6 sts

Turn it right side out if it isn't: push the hook in at the open end and out
through the tip, catch the magic-ring tail and pull it back through the
horn. Hold the open edges and pull on the tail and the horn flips. Push the
tail back inside with the hook and trim it.

Close: fold the opening flat and sc 3 through both layers. — 3 sts

Fasten off, leaving a tail of 15–20 cm (Ours for the length; the designer
says "a little tail"). It is for firming up the horn on the head later.

## Head, body and tail

Main colour throughout, one piece. R1–R15 is the head, R16–R32 the body,
R33–R47 the tail.

### Head

**R1:** 8 sc in a magic ring, pull closed. — 8 sts

This is the tip of the nose; the head is built from the nose backwards.

**R2:** inc in each st around. — 16 sts

**R3–4:** sc in each st around (2 rounds). — 16 sts

**R5:** 6 sc, inc 4 times, 6 sc. — 20 sts

The four increases are the top of the snout. Put markers in the two middle
stitches of this round, the 10th and 11th, and leave them in: they mark the
centre of the face, and everything else is lined up against them.

**R6:** [3 sc, inc] 5 times. — 25 sts

**R7:** 5 sc, inc 2 times, 1 sc, dec, 1 sc, inc 3 times, 1 sc, dec, 1 sc,
inc 2 times, 5 sc. — 30 sts

**R8:** dec, 26 sc, dec. — 28 sts

**R9:** [3 sc, inc] 7 times. — 35 sts

**R10:** [2 sc, dec] 3 times, 11 sc, [dec, 2 sc] 3 times. — 29 sts

**Line up:** R11 must start at the centre of the underside, directly below
the two face markers, or the ears and horns come out crooked. Look at the
head from the front and work shift stitches (the designer needed 2) until
the hook is there; move the marker.

**R11 (ears and horns):** 5 sc. Hold the first ear against the side of the
head, bottom edge along the next 5 stitches, and 5 sc through ear and head.
3 sc through the first horn and the head. 4 sc. 3 sc through the second horn
and the head. 5 sc through the second ear and the head. 4 sc. — 29 sts

The face markers now sit between the horns; take them out. Push the parts'
loose ends inside the head.

**R12:** inc, 28 sc. — 30 sts

If a horn flops, firm it now: thread its tail on the needle, go up into the
horn a couple of stitches, straight back down into the head beside it, and
repeat about 3 times. Then take the yarn inside the head, catch a stitch,
knot it and bury the end. Optional, but the designer shows the difference
and it is large.

**R13:** [3 sc, dec] 6 times. — 24 sts

**R14:** [2 sc, dec] 6 times. — 18 sts

**Stuff the head** now, before the eyes, so you can see where they will sit.
Then the **eyes**: between R5 and R6, on the sides of the snout, about 7
stitches apart, each in line with the horn and ear on its side. They look
too far apart at this stage; the face shaping at the end brings them in.
Push the washers on once you're happy.

**R15:** [1 sc, dec] 6 times. — 12 sts

### Body

Don't cut the yarn; the body grows out of the back of the head.

**R16:** [1 sc, inc] 6 times. — 18 sts

**R17:** sc in each st around. — 18 sts

From here to the end of the tail every round must start at the centre of the
belly, so that the wings land on the spine, the feet on the sides and the
tail's decreases on top. Check before each round that places a part or has
an asymmetric pattern, and work shift stitches when the marker has drifted.

**R18 (wings):** 6 sc. Take the first wing and decide how it should sit (the
designer has the webbing facing up and the wing lying along the back). Fold
it forward over the head so that its corner, where the dark half-circle
meets the light scalloped border, lies along the next 3 stitches. 3 sc
through wing and body: the first 2 through the dark part of the wing, the
third through the light border. Straight away the second wing, mirrored:
1 sc through its light border, then 2 through the dark part. 6 sc. — 18 sts

The two light corners meet in the middle of the back. Once the body is done
the wings fold back and lie along it.

**R19:** [3 sc, inc 2 times] 3 times, 3 sc. — 24 sts

**R20:** 2 sc, dec, 2 sc, inc 2 times, dec, 1 sc, inc 2 times, 1 sc, dec,
inc 2 times, 2 sc, dec, 2 sc. — 26 sts

Check the marker; the designer shifted one stitch here.

**R21 (front feet):** 1 sc. Hold a foot against the body with its seam along
the next 6 stitches and the toenails pointing towards the head, and 6 sc
through foot and body. 5 sc, inc 2 times, 5 sc. 6 sc through the second foot
and the body, toenails forward. 1 sc. — 28 sts

**R22:** 6 sc, inc 2 times, 12 sc, inc 2 times, 6 sc. — 32 sts

**R23:** 8 sc, inc, 6 sc, inc 2 times, 6 sc, inc, 8 sc. — 36 sts

**R24:** dec, 10 sc, dec, 8 sc, dec, 10 sc, dec. — 32 sts

**R25:** sc in each st around. — 32 sts

**R26 (back feet):** 1 sc. 6 sc through the third foot and the body, toenails
forward. 18 sc. 6 sc through the fourth foot and the body. 1 sc. — 32 sts

**R27:** 6 sc, dec, 6 sc, dec 2 times, 6 sc, dec, 6 sc. — 28 sts

**R28:** sc in each st around. — 28 sts

**Stuff the body** now; the tail starts narrowing next.

**R29:** 6 sc, [2 sc, dec] 4 times, 6 sc. — 24 sts

**R30:** 6 sc, [1 sc, dec] 4 times, 6 sc. — 20 sts

**R31:** 6 sc, dec 4 times, 6 sc. — 16 sts

**R32:** sc in each st around. — 16 sts

Top up the stuffing.

### Tail

Ten rounds, each one stitch fewer, with the single decrease on top of the
tail. That only works if the round starts underneath: keep the marker at the
centre of the underside and shift when it drifts. Stuff every couple of
rounds; the opening gets small fast.

**R33:** 7 sc, dec, 7 sc. — 15 sts

**R34:** 7 sc, dec, 6 sc. — 14 sts

**R35:** 6 sc, dec, 6 sc. — 13 sts

**R36:** 6 sc, dec, 5 sc. — 12 sts

**R37:** 5 sc, dec, 5 sc. — 11 sts

**R38:** 5 sc, dec, 4 sc. — 10 sts

**R39:** 4 sc, dec, 4 sc. — 9 sts

**R40:** 4 sc, dec, 3 sc. — 8 sts

**R41:** 3 sc, dec, 3 sc. — 7 sts

**R42:** 3 sc, dec, 2 sc. — 6 sts

**R43–47:** sc in each st around (5 rounds). — 6 sts

**Close and curl:** fasten off with a tail long enough to close the tip and
then run down one side of the tail; 40 cm is plenty (Ours for the length).
Push a little stuffing into the tip if it will go. Thread the tail on the
needle, weave through the front loops of the last round and pull tight.
Then decide which way the tail should curl, and weave the same yarn in and
out down that side of the tail, every other round, a good way up towards the
body. Pull, and the tail curls round. Knot it and bury the end in the body.

## Face

All with the needle, through the finished head.

**Nostrils:** accent colour. Go in at the side of the nose where a nostril
should be, leaving a tail hanging, and build a wrapped bar about two stitches
wide, as for the toenails, until it stands out. Take the needle under the
bottom of the nose to the matching spot on the other side and make the
second one. Come back to the starting tail, tie the two ends together and
bury them.

**Bringing the eyes in:** a scrap of the main colour. In at the top inner
corner of one eye, out at the top inner corner of the other; leave a tail.
Then back in at the bottom inner corner of that eye and out at the bottom
inner corner of the first. Pull the two ends until the eyes sit closer
together and the face looks right, tie them, trim, bury the ends.

**Eye highlights:** a scrap of white. In at the top centre of one eye, out at
its outer side. Re-thread the needle on the other end of the scrap, in at
the bottom centre of the same eye, out at the same spot on the outer side.
The white now curves round the outer half of the eye. Tie the ends together
and bury them. Repeat on the other eye.

## Spikes

Accent colour, surface crocheted in one line straight down the middle, from
the top of the head to the tip of the tail, passing between the horns and
between the wings. Insert the hook at the top centre of the head just in
front of the horns, pull up a loop and make 1 sc. Then [3 dc in one spot,
sl st in each of the next 2 spots] all the way down; each group of 3 dc is
a spike. Fasten off at the tip of the tail and weave in both ends.

The recording cut out before the designer finished this row (her phone
died), so the video never shows the spikes completed; the thumbnail does.

## Finishing

Nothing to sew. Check that every part's ends are inside, settle the wings
along the back, and give the tail its curl. Chenille ends can be pulled
through the body with the hook and trimmed flush.

## Tips from the source

- **Safety eyes are not safe for small children**: they can come out and be
  swallowed. For a dragon going to a child under about three, embroider the
  eyes with black yarn or use felt.
- **Keep the start of the round centred under the belly.** The whole pattern
  depends on it; if a part or the tail comes out crooked, this is why. Shift
  stitches are the fix, and the marker goes in the last stitch of the round.
- **Know the front of each foot.** The six increases of R3 are the front, and
  it bulges where the back is flatter. The closing seam must run parallel to
  it, and the toenails go on it.
- **Stuff the head before placing the eyes**, so you see the real shape.
- **The eyes look too far apart** until the shaping thread at the end pulls
  them together. Trust it.
- **Floppy horns** are worth the three stitches with the horn's tail. The
  designer shows one firmed and one not, and the difference is obvious.
- **Horns too fiddly?** Drop a hook size for them.
- **Wing ends:** knot the colour-change ends together, then push every end
  between the layers; the wing is two layers thick, so there's room.
- **Attaching a wing**, there are no particular stitches to aim for: grab
  wherever the hook will go through, and keep the three sc evenly spaced.
- **Toenails forward** whenever you attach a foot.

## Variations

- Horns in any colour; the designer chose white on a whim.
- (Ours, untested) Embroidered eyes for a small child: a flat black circle in
  satin stitch between R5 and R6, with the white highlight stitched over its
  outer edge the same way. The "bringing the eyes in" thread can be skipped.
- (Ours, untested) Two-tone wings the other way round, main colour inside
  and accent border, so the dragon reads as one colour with dark trim.
