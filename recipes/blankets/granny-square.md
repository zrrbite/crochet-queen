## Materials

- Yarn, one colour or several (see variations)
- A hook to match
- Tapestry needle, scissors

## Gauge

Not needed for a single square. If you're making squares to join, make them
all with the same yarn, hook and number of rounds so they match.

## Before you start

- **A cluster** here means 3 dc worked together into the same space.
- **You work into the spaces, not the stitches.** Each cluster goes into a
  gap under a chain from the round below, with the hook pushed through the
  gap itself.
- **Corners** (the ch-3 spaces) get 2 clusters with ch 3 between them.
  **Side spaces** (the ch-1 spaces) get 1 cluster. Every cluster is followed
  by ch 1 before you move to the next space.
- **Each round starts with ch 6**, which counts as 1 dc plus the corner's ch 3.
  The round ends back in that first corner with 2 dc, which together with the
  first 3 chains make that corner's second cluster. Joining here puts the seam
  in a corner, where it's hard to spot.
- Don't turn between rounds.

## Pattern

**Start:** ch 4, sl st into the first ch to make a ring.

**R1:** ch 6 (counts as 1 dc + ch 3). Into the ring: [3 dc, ch 3] 3 times,
then 2 dc. Sl st into the 3rd ch of the ch 6 to join.
— 4 clusters, 4 corner spaces

**R2:** sl st into the next corner space. Ch 6 (counts as 1 dc + ch 3), 3 dc in
the same space, ch 1. In each of the next 3 corner spaces: 3 dc, ch 3, 3 dc,
ch 1. Back in the first corner: 2 dc. Sl st into the 3rd ch of the ch 6.
— 8 clusters: 2 in each corner

**R3:** sl st into the next corner space. Ch 6, 3 dc in the same space, ch 1.
\*In the side space: 3 dc, ch 1. In the corner: 3 dc, ch 3, 3 dc, ch 1\*;
rep from \* twice more. In the last side space: 3 dc, ch 1. Back in the first
corner: 2 dc. Sl st into the 3rd ch of the ch 6.
— 12 clusters: each side has 1 cluster between its corners

**R4:** the same, with 2 side spaces per side, so 2 single clusters between
each pair of corners. Join as before. — 16 clusters

**Bigger:** every round works the same way and has one more side space per
side than the last. Each round adds 4 clusters.

**Fasten off:** ch 1, cut the yarn, and pull the tail through. Weave in both
ends on the back.

## Tips from the source

- **Count the spaces, not the stitches.** Before each round, find the four
  corners (the bigger ch-3 gaps) and you'll always know where you are.
- **One colour or many:** a colour change every round looks very pretty, but
  a solid square is lovely too.
- Make them as big or small as the project needs.

## Variations

- **Striped square:** change colour at the start of a round. Fasten off the
  old colour after the join, then join the new one with a sl st in a corner
  space and start that round's ch 6 there. See
  [changing colour](../../knowledge/techniques/colour-changes.md).
- **Projects:** a blanket, a cushion cover, a tote bag (the designer has a
  separate video on making one from small squares), or a single big square as
  a baby blanket.
