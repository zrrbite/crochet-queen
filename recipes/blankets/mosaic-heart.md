# Mosaic heart

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2GPfHNqD-_U"><img src="https://img.youtube.com/vi/2GPfHNqD-_U/maxresdefault.jpg" alt="A light pink square with a dark pink heart and fringed sides, next to its chart and a half-finished second square" width="480"></a>

*The video's thumbnail; click to watch.*

A square with a heart on it, 26 stitches wide and 27 rows tall, worked in
overlay mosaic crochet: one colour per row, cut at the end of every row,
with double crochets dropped two rows down to hide the other colour wherever
the heart isn't. The designer wrote it as a first mosaic project. The stitches
are the basic three and every row is symmetric and short, which is why this
is **intermediate** rather than experienced: what's new is the technique's
rules (sc in the back loop, drops into the front loop two rows down, a
stitch skipped behind each drop), changing colour at every row, and keeping
your place on a chart.

## Materials

- Yarn in two colours: **A** for the background, **B** for the heart. A
  little of each; 20 g apiece is plenty (Ours, estimate)
- 4 mm hook
- Scissors: you cut the yarn at the end of every row
- Tapestry needle, only if you weave in rather than fringe or border

## Gauge

Not critical for a single square. If you make several to join, use the same
yarn and hook for all of them (Ours).

## Before you start

- **Every row is worked from the right side, right to left.** Never turn.
  At the end of each row: ch 1, cut the yarn, pull the tail through and
  tighten. Start the next row back at the right edge with the other colour.
- **Colours alternate.** Odd rows are A (background), even rows are B
  (heart). The row's colour is given with each row below.
- In the pattern lines:
  - **edge sc** is a sc under **both** loops. It's the first and last stitch
    of every row and never part of the picture. The first one is how you
    join the new colour: insert the hook under both loops of the first
    stitch, pull up a loop of the new colour, and finish the sc.
  - **sc** is a sc in the **back loop only**.
  - **dc** is a drop: a dc into the **front loop** of the stitch two rows
    below, the one in the colour on your hook. Skip the stitch directly
    behind it.
- **Every row has 26 stitches.** Count at the end of each one; an extra
  stitch means you worked into the stitch behind a drop or into the last
  row's finishing chain.
- Rows 2 and 4 are plain sc rows in B, and row 3 covers row 2 completely.
  That's how the fabric starts: the heart appears from row 5.
- Leave tails of about 5 cm at each end, or 10 cm if you want a fringe like
  the designer's.

## Border

The designer's square has no border: the tails are left on both sides and
trimmed into a fringe, which is how the Ravelry photos show it.

For a square to join or to use on its own without a fringe (Ours, after the
way Tinna hides her ends; the detail isn't in either video): with A, join in
a corner and sc round the whole square. Along the top and bottom, one sc in
each stitch; down each side, one sc into each row end, laying the tails
along the edge and working the sc over them so they're caught inside. Put
3 sc in each corner. A second round of sc, or whatever border you like,
hides anything that still shows. Trim the tails close afterwards.

## Finishing

- Fringe: trim the tails to one length. Pull each one gently first so the
  edge stitch it belongs to is snug.
- Border: trim the caught tails, and weave in the border's own two ends
  (see [weaving in ends](../../knowledge/techniques/finishing.md#weaving-in-ends)).
- A light steam or wet block squares it up and flattens the drops (Ours).

## Tips from the source

- **The pattern is what you choose to reveal.** Every drop hides a stitch of
  the other colour; every sc leaves one showing for the next row to build
  on. Thinking of it that way makes the chart make sense.
- **The first stitch of each row tells you where you are.** The edge
  stitches alternate colour, so you can find your row on the chart at a
  glance.
- **Rows 2 and 4 disappear**, and the two B stitches left showing in row 5
  are the bottom tip of the heart. Everything grows from those two.
- **Each new row of the heart is two stitches wider on each side** up to
  row 14, which is why the counts step down by two: 11, 9, 7, 5, 3, 1.
- From Tinna's guide: a drop always goes into a stitch of the colour on your
  hook; tighten the finishing chain so you won't work into it; count rows
  from the back, where they're plain stripes; and pick colours with real
  contrast.

## Variations

- **Swap the colours** so the heart is light on dark (Ours, untested).
- **Several squares joined** make a blanket or cushion front; use a border on
  each so the ends are inside (Ours, untested).
- **Different colours per heart.** Only B shows in the heart, so change B
  from square to square, or even between even rows for a striped heart
  (Ours, untested; Tinna's scrap purse changes colour every row of a
  mosaic pattern, which is where the idea comes from).
- The designer sells further mosaic charts; any chart of 26 stitches and 27
  rows can replace this one with the same rows-and-edges routine.
