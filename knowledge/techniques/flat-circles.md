# Flat circles

Working in the round to make a flat disc: coasters, the bottom of a basket,
the top of a hat, the centre of a mandala.

## The rule

Start with **N** stitches in the first round. Every round after that adds
another **N**, spread evenly, so round *r* has **N × r** stitches.

Round 2 doubles every stitch. After that, each round puts one more plain
stitch between increases than the round before:

| Round | Repeat (N times)          | Stitches |
| ----- | ------------------------- | -------- |
| 1     | N sts into the ring       | N        |
| 2     | inc                       | 2N       |
| 3     | 1 st, inc                 | 3N       |
| 4     | 2 sts, inc                | 4N       |
| 5     | 3 sts, inc                | 5N       |
| *r*   | (*r* − 2) sts, inc        | *r* × N  |

One way to see it: the circle is a pie cut into N slices. Each round, every
slice gets one stitch wider.

## How many stitches to start with

Taller stitches need more stitches in round 1 to lie flat, because the circle
gets wider faster per round.

| Stitch | Usual N |
| ------ | ------- |
| sc     | 6       |
| hdc    | 8       |
| dc     | 10–12   |

These are starting points. Yarn and tension shift them a little, and a
pattern that's been tested should be trusted over this table.

## Joined or continuous rounds

**Joined:** end each round with a sl st into its first stitch, then make a
starting chain for the next. Each round is a clean ring. The join leaves a
faint seam. Best for tall stitches like dc, where a spiral makes a visible
step at the end.

**Continuous (spiral):** no join and no starting chain; go straight from the
last stitch of one round into the first stitch of the next. No seam, but you
must mark the first stitch of each round with a stitch marker or you will
lose count. Usual for sc and for amigurumi.

## Does the starting chain count?

Traditionally a dc round starts with ch 3, and that chain counts as the first
dc. The catch is that a chain is thinner than a real stitch, so the seam can
gap.

Some designers make a shorter chain (ch 2) that **doesn't count**, purely for
height, and work a real dc into the same stitch the chain comes out of. If you
do this:

- work the first stitch of the round into the stitch at the base of the chain;
- join into the top of that first real stitch, **not** into the chain;
- never work into the chain on the next round.

Read the pattern carefully: mixing the two approaches is how a round ends up
one stitch out.

## Troubleshooting

**It cups or domes like a bowl.** Too few stitches for the size. Count the last
round. If the count is right, your tension may be tightening as you go: relax
your grip, or go up a hook size. A slight dome is normal and usually blocks
out (see [finishing](finishing.md#blocking)).

**It ruffles or goes wavy.** Too many stitches. Count; you may have increased
twice in a slice. Can also be tension loosening on later rounds.

**It's turning hexagonal.** Increases stacked directly above each other make
corners. Stagger them: on alternate rounds, split the plain stitches either
side of the increase, e.g. instead of `2 dc, inc` work `1 dc, inc, 1 dc`.
Same count, corners blurred.

**There's a gap at the seam.** Try a non-counting starting chain (above). Or
split the first increase of the round: 1 dc at the start, and its second dc
into that same stitch just before you join.

**Lost count mid-round.** Put a stitch marker in the first stitch of each
round, and check the repeat every slice or two. Missing one increase is enough
to start a cone.
