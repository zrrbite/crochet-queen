# Mosaic crochet

Two-colour patterns that look like colourwork but never have two colours on
the hook at once. Every row is a single colour, and the pattern appears
because some stitches are tall enough to hide the row underneath them. It
needs only three stitches: chain, single crochet and double crochet.

## What it is

Rows alternate colour: A, B, A, B. In each row there are two kinds of stitch:

- a **single crochet**, which is short and shows the row's own colour;
- a **double crochet "drop"**, worked down into the row *two* below (which is
  the same colour as the yarn on the hook). It stands in front of the row in
  between, the other colour, and hides it.

Where you drop decides what shows. A row of colour B that is entirely covered
by colour A's drops vanishes; a run of B stitches that the next A row leaves
alone stays visible and becomes part of the motif. The front is the picture,
the back is plain stripes, one per row.

The repo's [spike stitch](../stitches/stitch-patterns.md#spike-stitch) is
the same idea with a sc; mosaic uses a dc because it is exactly two rows tall.

## Overlay and inset

**Overlay mosaic** is what both source videos teach and what this page
describes. Every row is worked from the **right side**, right to left, and
cut at the end; the next row starts again at the right edge in the other
colour. Because any row can drop anywhere, the motifs can be as detailed as
a chart allows. The price is two tails per row, which are dealt with at the
end (see [the ends and the border](#the-ends-and-the-border)).

**Inset mosaic** (Ours; neither video covers it) is the other style. It is
worked back and forth with turning, each colour for two rows at a time, so
there is nothing to cut. The gaps for the drops are made with chain spaces
instead of left-over front loops, and the drops go into those. No ends, but
simpler motifs, and its charts read differently; nothing in this repo uses
it yet.

## The stitches

**Single crochet in the back loop only.** Every plain stitch of the pattern
is a sc worked under the back loop only (see
[working in back or front loops](../stitches/stitch-patterns.md#working-in-back-or-front-loops)).
This is the whole trick: it leaves the front loop free, standing on the
right side of the fabric, for a later row to drop into.

**The dc drop.** Where the chart has an X:

1. Yarn over. Find the stitch you would normally work into next, in the row
   just below.
2. Follow that stitch straight down one more row. The front loop you left
   there is your target; it is in the colour that is on your hook.
3. Go under that front loop, from front to back, and work an ordinary
   [double crochet](../stitches/basic-stitches.md#double-crochet-dc).
4. **Skip the stitch directly behind the drop** in the row below. It is
   covered; working into it as well would add a stitch.

With two colours, a drop always goes into a stitch of the same colour as the
yarn on the hook. If the loop you are about to go under is the other colour,
you are a row out.

**Edge stitches.** The first and last stitch of every row are sc worked
under **both** loops, so the sides are firm. They are not part of the
pattern: a chart either shows them as its first and last columns, or the
pattern tells you to add them outside the repeat. Tinna starts the row by
inserting the hook under both loops of the first stitch, pulling up a loop,
ch 1, then sc into the same stitch; Jessie simply joins with a sc under both
loops. Either works. The last stitch of the row is a sc under both loops of
the last stitch, never into the row's finishing chain.

## One row, start to finish

1. Right side up, the row's start at your right (left-handers mirror
   everything).
2. Take the other colour from the one you just finished. Leave a tail (about
   5 cm if it will go inside a border, longer if you want a fringe) and join
   with the edge stitch, under both loops of the first stitch.
3. Work across: sc in the back loop where the chart is blank, a drop where
   it has an X.
4. Edge stitch under both loops of the last stitch.
5. Ch 1, cut the yarn, pull the tail through and **tighten the chain hard**.
   That chain only secures the row; it is never worked into, and a tight one
   is easy to ignore.
6. Don't turn. Go back to the right edge for the next row.

The stitch count is the same in every row, and the first row of any piece is
plain sc into a chain of one more than the count.

## Reading a chart

- One square is one stitch, one line of squares is one row. Rows are
  numbered from the bottom; you work upwards.
- Every row is one colour, and the rows alternate. Usually odd rows are
  colour A and even rows colour B; a chart marks each row's colour at its
  edge, with a coloured square or a letter.
- **X means a dc drop. Blank means sc in the back loop.** The edge columns,
  if the chart shows them, are the both-loops sc.
- Overlay charts are read **right to left on every row**, because every row
  starts at the right edge. (Symmetric rows, like the heart's, read the same
  either way. Inset charts are read back and forth like any flat chart;
  Ours.)
- The squares are coloured to look like the finished fabric, so a square's
  colour is **not** always the row's colour. A dark square without an X in a
  light row is a sc that the next row's drops will cover; a run of blank
  squares in the row's own colour is a run the next row leaves alone.
- A chart never puts an X directly above an X. If you find yourself dropping
  into a dc, you are a column off.

**Keeping your place.** Both edge stitches show their row's colour, so from
the front the edges are a row counter: light, dark, light, dark. From the
back the stripes are even easier to count. Jessie's tip is that the edge
stitch of the row you are on tells you which chart row you are on.

## The ends and the border

Overlay leaves two tails on every row. Don't weave them in one by one;
neither designer does:

- **A border over them.** Tinna works a double border round her blankets
  and the ends live inside it. She shows it in a later lesson, not this one.
  One way (Ours): sc round the whole piece, and when you go down a side, lay
  the tails along the edge and work the sc over them, as you would carry a
  yarn. A second round of sc hides whatever still shows. Trim the tails
  close once the border is done.
- **A fringe.** Jessie's heart simply keeps its tails, trimmed even, as a
  fringe down both sides. Tinna makes a twisted fringe on scarves.

## Tension

Tinna's only rule is a hook that suits the yarn. Beyond that (Ours): use the
same hook for both colours; a dc is exactly two rows tall, so a drop should
lie flat on the fabric, neither pulling the row below up nor dragging the
current row down. Pull the first loop of the dc up to the height of the row
you are working, as for a spike stitch, then finish it normally. If the
columns of drops pucker, you are pulling that first loop tight; if they bag,
it is too loose. Keep the sc rows even too, because they set the fabric's
width.

Pick two colours with **strong contrast**, and light enough to see your
stitches, or the pattern blurs.

## Common mistakes

- **Sc in both loops.** There is then no front loop to drop into. Only the
  two edge stitches go under both loops.
- **Working the stitch behind a drop.** Each one adds a stitch. The count
  must be the same in every row; count whenever a row ends.
- **Working into the row's finishing chain** at the start of the next row:
  one stitch too many. Tighten that chain so it doesn't look like a stitch.
- **Dropping into the wrong row.** A dc into the row just below hides
  nothing. The target is two rows down and the same colour as your hook.
- **Turning.** Every row starts at the same edge, right side facing.
- **Losing the colour order.** Rows strictly alternate. If two tails of the
  same colour are next to each other on the edge, undo a row.

## A practice swatch: Apache tears

The swatch Tinna teaches with, from a 1970s pattern. The repeat is 6
stitches: 1 drop, 5 sc, and every row the drop moves one stitch to the left.
Three repeats plus 2 edge stitches is 20 stitches; chain one more.

**R1 (A):** ch 21. Sc in the 2nd ch from the hook and in each ch across.
Ch 1, cut. — 20 sts

**R2 (B):** edge sc, 18 sc, edge sc. — 20 sts

**R3 (A):** edge sc, [1 dc, 5 sc] 3 times, edge sc. — 20 sts

**R4 (B):** edge sc, 1 sc, [1 dc, 5 sc] twice, 1 dc, 4 sc, edge sc. — 20 sts

**R5 (A):** edge sc, 2 sc, [1 dc, 5 sc] twice, 1 dc, 3 sc, edge sc. — 20 sts

**R6 (B):** edge sc, 3 sc, [1 dc, 5 sc] twice, 1 dc, 2 sc, edge sc. — 20 sts

**R7 (A):** edge sc, 4 sc, [1 dc, 5 sc] twice, 1 dc, 1 sc, edge sc. — 20 sts

**R8 (B):** edge sc, 5 sc, [1 dc, 5 sc] twice, 1 dc, edge sc. — 20 sts

**R9 (A):** as R3. The drop that would fall off the left end comes back in
at the right. — 20 sts

**R10 (B):** as R4, and so on. — 20 sts

The rule for any row: if the previous row ended with a drop, this one starts
with one. The fifth sc of every group always lands in the previous row's
drop. Every sc goes in the back loop, every dc goes two rows down into the
front loop, and that is the whole technique.
