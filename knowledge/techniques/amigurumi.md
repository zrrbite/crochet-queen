# Amigurumi

Small stuffed toys, mostly worked in tight single crochet in a spiral. The
Japanese word roughly means "crocheted stuffed toy".

## Spiral rounds and the stitch marker

Amigurumi is usually worked in **continuous rounds**: no slip-stitch join, no
starting chain. The last stitch of one round runs straight into the first of
the next. That means nothing in the fabric shows where a round ends, so:

- put a stitch marker in the last stitch (or the first; patterns differ) of
  each round;
- move it up every round;
- count the round's stitches before you move it.

To find the first stitch of a new round, count back from the marker.

## Starting

- **A circle:** 6 sc in a [magic ring](magic-ring.md), pulled tight so there's
  no hole.
- **An oval:** chain a few stitches and work along **both sides** of the
  chain. Single crochet down one side, 2 or more stitches in the last chain to
  turn the corner, then back up the other side of the chain. Used for bodies
  that are wider than they are deep, like
  [Waffle the bear](../../recipes/amigurumi/waffle-the-bear.md) and the
  [leggy frog](../../recipes/amigurumi/leggy-frog.md).

## Tension: tight

The fabric must be dense enough that stuffing can't show between stitches.
Use a hook **one or two sizes smaller** than the yarn's label suggests. If you
can see fibre fill through the gaps, go down another size.

## Right side out

A piece worked in the round naturally curls with its wrong side outwards.
Once it's deep enough to hold, about when it starts to form a bowl, push it
**right side out**: the V-shaped stitches go outside, the little horizontal
bars inside. Do it early on small pieces like arms and ears; they get very
hard to turn later.

## Shaping

Increases grow the piece out, plain rounds go straight, decreases bring it
back in. Use the [invisible decrease](../stitches/increases-and-decreases.md#invisible-decrease-invdec)
for a smooth surface. A ball shape that looks hexagonal mid-way through
smooths out once the increases stop.

## Stuffing

- Polyester fibre fill (toy stuffing) is the usual choice.
- **Stuff before the opening gets small**, usually a few rounds before the
  end. Top up again just before the last round.
- Pull the fill into small tufts and layer them in from the bottom up, so it
  sits evenly with no lumps.
- Firm or squishy is your choice. Firm holds its shape and stands up; squishy
  is cuddlier.
- Scraps of the same yarn work for tiny pieces like a muzzle.

## Safety eyes

Plastic eyes with a post that goes through the fabric and a washer that
locks on from the inside.

- **Place them before the piece is closed**, while you can still reach
  inside. Most patterns say between which rounds, and how many stitches apart.
- **Check placement before pushing the washer on.** Once it's on, it's very
  hard to get off.
- **Not for babies or small children.** They are a choking hazard if they come
  out. For a toy that goes to a child under about 3, embroider the eyes with
  black yarn or use felt.

## Closing a piece

1. Cut the yarn, leaving 20–25 cm.
2. Thread it on a tapestry needle and pass it through the **front loops** of
   each stitch in the last round.
3. Pull tight; the hole cinches shut like a drawstring bag.
4. Push the needle down through the centre and out somewhere else on the
   body, then cut the yarn flush with the surface. The end disappears inside.

## Sewing parts on

- **Pin everything first** and look at it from a distance before sewing.
  Placement is what gives a toy its expression.
- **Whip stitch:** needle under a stitch of the body, then through the edge of
  the part, and repeat around. Flattened parts (ears, arms) are sewn by their
  open edge; open cup-shaped parts (muzzles) by their last round.
- A **bent-tip tapestry needle** makes it much easier to catch a little of the
  body's fabric.
- Finish each part by going into the body and out somewhere else, then cut
  flush, as for closing.

## Faces

- **Nose:** satin stitch with black embroidery thread or yarn: lots of
  straight stitches side by side, fanning from a point, to fill a triangle.
- **Mouth:** one or two long straight stitches. Catch the middle down for a
  smile.
- **Cheeks:** a short piece of pink yarn pulled through one stitch wide, ends
  tied together inside.
- Do the face while the piece is still open, so the ends can be tied on the
  inside.

## No-sew construction

Some designs avoid sewing entirely: arms and legs are crocheted straight off
the body as you go, and the bottom is closed by folding it flat and slip
stitching through both layers. See the
[leggy frog](../../recipes/amigurumi/leggy-frog.md).
