# Garments

What changes when the thing has to fit a person.

## Gauge swatch

For a coaster, gauge doesn't matter. For a sweater, a gauge that's 10% off
makes a sweater that's 10% too big or too small, which is a whole size.

1. With the yarn and hook the pattern gives, chain enough for about 15 cm
   and work about 15 cm in the pattern's main stitch.
2. Block it the way you'll block the garment (below). Stitches relax when
   washed, and a swatch measured dry and unblocked lies.
3. Lay it flat and count the stitches and rows across 10 cm in the middle,
   away from the edges.
4. Compare with the pattern's gauge. **More** stitches than stated means your
   stitches are smaller: go up a hook size. **Fewer**: go down. Match the
   stitch count first; a row count that's slightly off is easier to fix by
   working more or fewer rows.

## Ease and sizing

**Ease** is the difference between the garment and the body it goes on.
Negative ease (garment smaller than the body) is fitted and stretches on;
zero ease skims; positive ease is relaxed. Oversized cardigans and sweaters
often have 15–25 cm of positive ease at the chest.

Patterns give either finished measurements or "to fit" sizes, or both. Choose
by the chest measurement plus the ease you want. The surest way: measure a
sweater or hoodie that fits the way you like, flat, and match its chest width
(doubled) and length.

## Measuring yourself

- **Chest:** around the fullest part, tape level.
- **Upper arm:** around the widest part.
- **Sleeve length:** from the underarm to the wrist, arm slightly bent.
- **Body length:** from the underarm to where you want the hem.
- **Head:** around the forehead and the widest part of the back of the head,
  for hats.

## Construction

- **Top-down raglan:** starts at the neck as a ring and grows with increases
  at four "raglan lines" that run from neck to underarm. At the underarm the
  sleeve stitches are set aside and the body continues; the sleeves are
  worked afterwards from the live stitches. One piece, no seams, and you can
  try it on as you go.
- **Hexagon cardigan:** two big granny hexagons, each folded in half to make
  a front, half the back and a sleeve. Joined up the centre back, then the
  body and sleeves are lengthened in the round.
- **Pieces:** a front, a back and two sleeves worked flat, blocked, then
  seamed. More sewing, but each piece is simple.
- **Bottom-up in the round:** a tube from the hem up, divided at the
  underarms for a yoke.

## Trying on as you go

Anything worked from the top down can be tried on. Before you do, slip the
live loop onto a locking stitch marker so it can't unravel. Check the yoke
depth (the underarm should sit at the underarm, not below), the body length
and the sleeve length against your measurements rather than against the
pattern's numbers.

## Ribbing

Crochet ribbing is either:

- **Sideways BLO rows:** rows of sc or hdc worked in back loops only, back
  and forth, making a strip with ridges running across it. The strip is
  stretchy along the ridges. Work it as long as the cuff or hem needs, then
  sew or slip stitch its ends together into a band and work the garment
  stitches along its edge, or attach it afterwards. See
  [stitch patterns](../stitches/stitch-patterns.md#working-in-back-or-front-loops).
- **Post-stitch rounds:** alternating FPdc and BPdc. Less stretchy, deeper
  texture, worked in the direction the garment already goes.

## Seaming

- **Whip stitch:** through both edges, over and over. Quick, slightly visible.
- **Mattress stitch:** on the right side, catching one bar from each edge in
  turn and pulling snug every few stitches. The seam closes up and vanishes.
- **Slip stitch or sc seam:** crocheting the two edges together. Makes a
  ridge; done on the right side it's a decorative line, on the wrong side it's
  hidden but bulkier.

Pin or clip the edges together first, matching rows to rows.

## Blocking a garment

- **Natural fibres** (wool, cotton, blends): wet-block. Soak in cool water,
  squeeze out in a towel without wringing, lay out flat to the finished
  measurements, pin the edges, leave to dry.
- **Acrylic:** wet-blocking does little. Steam-block instead: hold a steam
  iron or steamer a few centimetres above the fabric, never touching, and let
  the steam relax it. Too close or too long permanently flattens it.

Block pieces before seaming; block one-piece garments once, at the end.

## Yarn amounts

Patterns give yardage or grams per size. Buy one ball more than that, from the
**same dye lot** (the number on the label), because lots differ slightly in
colour. To estimate for a substitution: weigh your blocked swatch, divide by
its area, and multiply by a rough area for the garment.
