# Changing colour

## Mid-row or mid-round

Change on the **last step of the stitch before** the new colour. Work that
stitch in the old colour until two loops are left on the hook, then yo with
the new colour and pull it through. The next stitch starts cleanly in the new
colour.

## At the join of a joined round

Make the joining sl st with the new colour: insert the hook into the first
stitch of the round, catch the new colour, and pull it through. That one
slip stitch both closes the old round and starts the new colour.
The [scrunchie](../../recipes/accessories/scrunchie.md) edge works this way.

## Starting fresh in a new place

When you've fastened off and want the new colour somewhere else (a different
corner of a [granny square](../../recipes/blankets/granny-square.md), say):
make a slip knot in the new colour, insert the hook where you want to start,
pull a loop through, and carry on with the round's starting chain.

## The tails

- Leave about 10–15 cm of each colour.
- Tie them together loosely if they keep slipping, but still weave them in;
  the knot alone isn't enough.
- Quicker: **work over the tails.** Lay them along the top of the stitches
  you're about to work into and crochet around them for 5 or 6 stitches.
  They end up hidden inside the stitches; trim what's left.

## Stripes in a spiral

Changing colour in a continuous round leaves a visible step where the round
ends, because the spiral never really closes. For neat stripes, use joined
rounds, or accept the step and put it at the back.
