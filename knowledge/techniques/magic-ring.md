# Magic ring

Also called the magic circle or magic loop. An adjustable starting loop for
work in the round: you crochet the first round into it, then pull the tail
and the centre closes with no hole.

It's fiddly the first few times. If it fights you, use the
[chain ring](#alternative-chain-ring) below. It works fine and leaves only a
small hole.

## Making it

1. Hold the yarn tail against your palm. Wrap the working yarn around your
   index and middle fingers so it crosses over the tail, making an **X** on
   the back of your fingers.
2. Slide the hook under the strand nearer your fingertips, catch the other
   strand, and pull it back under to make a loop on the hook.
3. Yo and pull through that loop, to lock it. This is not a stitch.
4. Slip the ring off your fingers, keeping hold of where the strands cross.
5. Make the starting chain your pattern asks for, then work the first round's
   stitches **into the ring**, over both the ring and the tail.
6. When the round's stitches are in, pull the tail. The ring shrinks and the
   centre closes. Pull it firmly, then join the round as the pattern says.

## Securing the tail

The tail is the only thing holding the centre shut. When you weave it in,
take it through the back of the first-round stitches, then change direction
at least once so it can't work loose and let the centre open.

## Alternative: chain ring

Ch 3 (or 4 for a larger first round), sl st into the first chain to make a
ring, then work the first round into the centre of that ring. The centre
won't close up as tightly as a magic ring, but it's easier to start.
