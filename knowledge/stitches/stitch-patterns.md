# Stitch patterns

Combinations of the basic stitches that patterns name as if they were one
stitch. US terms. These are the usual versions; a pattern's own definition
always wins, because some of these (cluster, shell, puff) vary from designer
to designer.

## Working in back or front loops

Looking down at a row, each stitch has a V on top. Normally the hook goes
under both strands of the V. **Back loop only (BLO)** means under just the
strand further from you; **front loop only (FLO)**, just the nearer one.
Either leaves the unused strand as a ridge on the fabric. Rows of sc or hdc
in BLO, worked back and forth, stretch like ribbing (see
[garments](../techniques/garments.md#ribbing)).

## Puff stitch

A soft, rounded bump.

1. Yo, insert into the stitch, yo, pull up a loop to the height of the row.
2. Repeat step 1 into the same stitch 2–4 more times (7–11 loops on hook).
3. Yo, pull through all loops. Ch 1 to close the top.

## Bobble

Several double crochets worked into one stitch and joined at the top.

1. [Yo, insert into the stitch, yo, pull up a loop, yo, pull through 2]
   4 or 5 times into the same stitch (5 or 6 loops on hook).
2. Yo, pull through all loops.

A bobble pops out towards the **back** of the row you're working. Patterns
usually place them on wrong-side rows so they show on the right side.

## Popcorn

Bigger and firmer than a bobble.

1. Work 4 or 5 complete dc into the same stitch.
2. Take the hook out of the loop. Insert it from front to back into the top
   of the **first** dc of the group, then catch the dropped loop and pull it
   through.

## Cluster

Stitches worked over neighbouring stitches and joined at the top, like a
decrease, e.g. `dc3tog` across 3 stitches. Note that a granny square's
"cluster" is different: 3 dc in one space, not joined. Check the pattern.

## Shell

Several stitches fanned out of one stitch or space, commonly 5 dc. The
stitches either side are usually skipped to make room. `(2 dc, ch 1, 2 dc)`
in one stitch is a small shell with a gap in the middle.

## V-stitch

`(dc, ch 1, dc)` in one stitch. On the next row, the V goes into the ch-1
space of the V below. Fast, open, drapey.

## Picot

A tiny point, mostly for edgings. Ch 3, then sl st into the first of those
3 chains (or into the stitch the chain came from, which makes it stand up
more).

## Front post and back post double crochet

Instead of going into the top of the stitch below, the hook goes **around its
post** (the vertical body of the stitch).

- **FPdc:** yo, insert the hook from the front, right to left behind the post
  and out to the front again, then finish a dc as usual. The stitch sits
  forward.
- **BPdc:** the same from the back: hook in from the back, around the front
  of the post, out to the back. The stitch sits back.

Alternating FPdc and BPdc makes a deep ribbing. All FPdc in a column, offset
across rows, makes cables and basketweave.

## Crossed double crochet

Skip 1 stitch, dc in the next, then dc into the skipped stitch, working over
the first dc. Makes an X.

## Spike stitch

A sc worked into a stitch one or more rows **below** the current row, with
the loop pulled up to the current height so the fabric doesn't pucker. In a
contrast colour it makes a long V. Mosaic crochet is built on this idea.

## Foundation single crochet

Makes the starting chain and the first row of sc in one go. Stretchier than a
chain and easier to work into.

1. Ch 2. Insert into the 2nd ch from the hook, yo, pull up a loop (2 loops).
2. Yo, pull through 1 loop: this makes the "chain" of the stitch.
3. Yo, pull through 2 loops: the sc. One foundation sc made.
4. For the next: insert the hook under the two strands of the chain you made
   in step 2, and repeat steps 2 and 3.

## Granny stripe

The granny square worked flat: rows of 3-dc groups with ch 1 between, each
row's groups going into the ch-1 spaces of the row below, so they sit offset
like bricks.
