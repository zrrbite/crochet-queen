# Yarn and hooks

## Yarn weights

Patterns name yarn weight differently by country. The Craft Yarn Council
number (0–7) is printed on most US labels; UK and Australian labels use
names or ply counts.

| CYC | US name              | UK / AU name          | Suggested hook |
| --- | -------------------- | --------------------- | -------------- |
| 0   | Lace                 | 1–2 ply               | 1.5–2.25 mm    |
| 1   | Super fine, fingering| 4 ply, sock           | 2.25–3.5 mm    |
| 2   | Fine, sport          | 5 ply                 | 3.5–4.5 mm     |
| 3   | Light, DK            | DK, 8 ply             | 4.5–5.5 mm     |
| 4   | Medium, worsted      | Aran, 10 ply          | 5.5–6.5 mm     |
| 5   | Bulky                | Chunky, 12 ply        | 6.5–9 mm       |
| 6   | Super bulky          | Super chunky, 14 ply  | 9–15 mm        |
| 7   | Jumbo                | Jumbo                 | 15 mm and up   |

The names don't line up exactly across countries; when in doubt, compare the
metres per 100 g on the label.

The label's hook size is a starting point, not a rule. Go **smaller** for a
dense, firm fabric (coasters, baskets, amigurumi, where stuffing mustn't show
through). Go **larger** for drape (shawls, garments).

## Fibres

| Fibre   | Good for                                   | Watch out for                                   |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Cotton  | coasters, dishcloths, baskets, summer tops | no stretch; heavy when wet; hard on the hands   |
| Acrylic | toys, blankets, practice; cheap, washable  | melts under heat: no trivets or pot holders     |
| Wool    | hats, garments; warm, blocks beautifully   | felts in a hot wash unless labelled superwash   |
| Bamboo  | drapey garments, shawls                    | slippery; stitches can stretch out              |

Blends behave in between.

## Other tools

- **Tapestry needle:** blunt tip, large eye, for weaving in ends.
- **Stitch markers:** split-ring or locking ones clip into a stitch to mark
  the start of a round or a repeat. Worth using even when you think you don't
  need them.
- **Small sharp scissors.**
